**Lesson 1: Role of an Agile Business Analyst**

**1. What is a Business Analyst?**

**A Business Analyst (BA) is a professional who acts as a bridge
between:**

-   **Business stakeholders**

-   **Customers/users**

-   **Product Owner**

-   **Development team**

-   **QA team**

-   **Data/AI teams**

**The BA\'s primary responsibility is to understand business problems,
convert them into clear requirements, and ensure the team builds the
right solution.**

**Simple definition:**

**A Business Analyst identifies business needs and translates them into
actionable requirements that technology teams can implement.**

**Example (Healthcare Domain)**

**Imagine a pharmaceutical company says:**

**\"We need an AI system to automatically process adverse drug event
reports.\"**

**Business problem:**

-   **Manual case processing takes 5 days**

-   **Too many errors**

-   **Compliance risk**

**BA investigates:**

**Business asks:**

**\"We want AI automation.\"**

**BA converts this into:**

**Feature:**

**\"AI should extract patient information from incoming ICSR
documents.\"**

**User Story:**

**As a Pharmacovigilance Specialist,\
I want AI to extract patient demographics automatically,\
so that I can reduce manual data entry time.**

**Acceptance Criteria:**

-   **System extracts patient age**

-   **System extracts gender**

-   **System identifies suspect drug**

-   **Extraction accuracy should be \>95%**

**This conversion is the BA\'s core responsibility.**

**5. Agile BA vs Product Owner vs Product Manager**

-   **Product strategy**

-   **Roadmap**

-   **Business success**

**\"We should build an AI Pharmacovigilance platform because companies

**Focus:**

**WHAT**

**Responsibilities:**

-   **Owns product backlog**

-   **Prioritizes features**

-   **Defines acceptance criteria**

-   **Works with Scrum team**

**Example:**

**\"The next sprint should include AI extraction module.\"**

**Focus:**

**HOW TO UNDERSTAND AND DEFINE**

**Responsibilities:**

-   **Analyze business needs**

-   **Break requirements into stories**

-   **Clarify details**

-   **Facilitate workshops**

-   **Support testing**

**\"AI extraction should capture patient age, drug name, reaction,

**Developer**

**Focus:**

**HOW TO BUILD**

**Creates:**

-   **Code**

-   **Architecture**

-   **Technical solution**

**Simple Comparison**

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Role**             **Main Question**
  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------

  **Product Owner**    **What should we build next?**

**Lesson 3: Requirement Gathering and Analysis Techniques for Agile BA**

**1. What is Requirement Gathering?**

Requirement gathering (also called **requirement elicitation**) is the
process of discovering:

-   What problem the business wants to solve

-   Who needs the solution

-   What users expect

-   What rules and constraints exist

A BA does not simply collect requirements.

A BA **investigates and discovers the real need behind the request**.

**Example**

Stakeholder says:

\"We need a dashboard.\"

A weak BA writes:

\"Create dashboard.\"

An Agile BA asks:

-   Who will use it?

-   What decisions will they make?

-   What information do they need?

-   How frequently will they use it?

-   What problem does it solve?

After analysis:

Actual requirement:

\"Safety managers need a real-time compliance dashboard to identify
overdue adverse event cases.\"

**2. Requirement Types**

Before gathering requirements, BA should understand different
requirement categories.

**A. Business Requirements**

High-level business objectives.

Question:

**Why are we building this?**

Example:

\"Reduce pharmacovigilance case processing time by 50%.\"

**B. User Requirements**

What users need to accomplish.

Question:

**Who needs what?**

Example:

\"Safety associates need to upload case documents.\"

**C. Functional Requirements**

What the system should do.

Question:

**What capability is needed?**

Examples:

-   System should allow PDF upload

-   System should extract patient details

-   System should generate reports

**D. Non-Functional Requirements**

How the system should perform.

Examples:

-   Response time \< 3 seconds

-   99.9% availability

-   Data encryption

-   Audit logging

**E. Regulatory Requirements**

Important in healthcare/pharma.

Examples:

-   Maintain audit trail

-   User access control

-   Electronic signatures

-   Data integrity

**7. Root Cause Analysis**

A BA should solve the actual problem, not just symptoms.

**Five Whys Technique**

Example:

Problem:

Case processing is delayed.

Why 1:

Why are cases delayed?

→ Because manual data entry takes time.

Why 2:

Why does data entry take time?

→ Information exists in multiple documents.

Why 3:

Why are documents not structured?

→ Reports arrive in different formats.

Why 4:

Why are formats inconsistent?

→ No standard intake process.

Why 5:

Why no standard process?

→ Legacy systems were created separately.

Root Cause:

Lack of standardized data intake.

Solution:

Not only automation, but standardized workflow.

**Lesson 4: Writing Effective User Stories as an Agile BA**

**1. What is a User Story?**

A **User Story** is a short description of a requirement written from
the perspective of the person who will use the system.

It explains:

-   Who needs something

-   What they need

-   Why they need it

A user story focuses on **value**, not technical implementation.

**Traditional Requirement**

Example:

\"System shall provide a search function where users can search adverse
event cases.\"

This describes the system.

**Agile User Story**

\"As a Safety Associate, I want to search adverse event cases by drug
name so that I can quickly find related cases.\"

This describes:

-   User

-   Need

-   Business value

**2. User Story Format**

The standard Agile format:

As a \<user\>

I want \<goal\>

So that \<benefit\>

**Example: Pharmacovigilance System**

As a Pharmacovigilance Specialist,

I want AI to extract adverse events from incoming reports,

So that I can reduce manual case processing time.

Breaking it down:

**User**

Who needs it?

→ Pharmacovigilance Specialist

**Goal**

What do they want?

→ AI extraction of adverse events

**Benefit**

Why?

→ Reduce manual processing effort

**3. Epic → Feature → User Story Hierarchy**

Large requirements cannot directly become stories.

Agile BA breaks them down.

**Level 1: Epic**

Large business capability.

Example:

AI Pharmacovigilance Platform

Too large for one sprint.

**Level 2: Feature**

Major functionality.

Example:

ICSR Case Processing

**Level 3: User Stories**

Small pieces of user value.

Example:

Feature:

ICSR Case Processing

Stories:

1.  

As a Safety Associate,

I want to upload case documents,

so that I can start case processing.

2.  

As a Safety Associate,

I want AI to extract patient details,

so that manual entry is reduced.

3.  

As a Medical Reviewer,

I want to review extracted information,

so that I can validate accuracy.

**4. User Story INVEST Principle**

A good user story follows INVEST.

**I -- Independent**

The story should work independently.

Bad:

\"Create dashboard.\"

Depends on:

-   Login

-   User management

-   Reports

Better:

Split into:

-   View dashboard

-   Filter dashboard

-   Export dashboard

**N -- Negotiable**

A story is not a fixed contract.

It should allow discussion.

Example:

Story:

\"User should receive notifications.\"

Discussion:

-   Email?

-   SMS?

-   In-app?

**V -- Valuable**

Every story should deliver user value.

Bad:

\"As a developer, I want database tables created.\"

No direct user value.

Better:

\"As a Safety Associate, I want cases stored securely so that I can
access them later.\"

**E -- Estimable**

The team should understand enough to estimate effort.

If unclear:

Story needs refinement.

**S -- Small**

A story should fit into a sprint.

Large:

\"Build complete AI platform.\"

Break into:

-   Upload documents

-   Extract data

-   Validate extraction

-   Generate reports

**T -- Testable**

The team should know how to verify completion.

Bad:

\"System should be user friendly.\"

Good:

\"User should complete case creation within five steps.\"

**Lesson 6: Acceptance Criteria, Business Rules, and Validation as an
Agile BA**

**1. Why Acceptance Criteria Matter**

A user story explains **what the user wants**.

But the development team needs more detail:

-   How will we know it is complete?

-   What behavior is expected?

-   What conditions must be satisfied?

This is the purpose of **Acceptance Criteria**.

**Simple Difference**

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **User Story**                      **Acceptance Criteria**
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Describes user need                 Defines conditions for success

  Explains value                      Explains expected behavior

  Written from user perspective       Written as testable conditions

  Answers \"Why?\"                    Answers \"How do we verify?\"
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Example**

**User Story:**

As a Pharmacovigilance Specialist,\
I want to upload adverse event documents,\
so that I can start case processing.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

-   User can upload PDF files

-   File size cannot exceed allowed limit

-   Invalid files show an error message

-   Uploaded documents receive a unique case ID

**2. Types of Acceptance Criteria**

A good Agile BA considers multiple categories.

**A. Functional Acceptance Criteria**

Defines what the system should do.

Example:

User Story:

\"User wants to search safety cases.\"

Acceptance Criteria:

✅ User can search using case ID\
✅ User can filter by date\
✅ Results display case status

**B. Data Acceptance Criteria**

Defines required data behavior.

Example:

AI extraction module:

Acceptance Criteria:

-   Patient age should be stored

-   Drug name should be mapped correctly

-   Missing values should be marked as unknown

**C. Validation Acceptance Criteria**

Defines rules and restrictions.

Example:

Case creation:

Rules:

-   Patient information is mandatory

-   Drug name is mandatory

-   Report date cannot be future date

**D. Security Acceptance Criteria**

Important for healthcare applications.

Example:

-   Only authorized users can access patient data

-   User activity must be logged

-   Sensitive data must be encrypted

**E. Performance Acceptance Criteria**

Defines system performance.

Example:

-   Dashboard should load within 3 seconds

-   AI extraction should complete within 60 seconds

**3. Writing Acceptance Criteria Using Given-When-Then**

This is the most common Agile format.

Also called:

**BDD (Behavior Driven Development)**

Format:

Given

Initial condition

When

Action occurs

Then

Expected result

**Example 1: Case Upload**

**User Story:**

As a Safety Associate,\
I want to upload an adverse event report.

Acceptance Criteria:

**Scenario: Successful upload**

**Given**

The user is logged into the system

**When**

The user uploads a valid PDF report

**Then**

The system should create a new case record

**Scenario: Invalid file**

**Given**

The user selects an unsupported file type

**When**

The upload button is clicked

**Then**

The system should display an error message

**4. Business Rules**

A business rule defines a policy, condition, or logic that the business
follows.

A BA must identify business rules because developers need them.

**Example: Pharmacovigilance**

Business requirement:

\"Identify serious adverse events.\"

Business rules:

A case is serious if:

-   Death occurred\
    OR

-   Life-threatening condition\
    OR

-   Hospitalization required\
    OR

-   Disability occurred

The BA documents this clearly.

**Business Rule Example**

**Rule:**

Seriousness Classification

IF:

Patient death = Yes

OR

Hospitalization = Yes

THEN:

Case classification = Serious

**5. Requirement vs Business Rule vs Acceptance Criteria**

Many beginners confuse these.

Example:

**Requirement:**

\"System should identify serious adverse events.\"

**Business Rule:**

\"A case is serious if hospitalization occurred.\"

**Acceptance Criteria:**

\"Given a case with hospitalization marked Yes, when the case is saved,
then seriousness should display as Serious.\"

A BA defines:

-   Required fields

-   Allowed values

-   Data formats

**Lesson 8: Agile BA and User Acceptance Testing (UAT)**

**1. What is User Acceptance Testing (UAT)?**

**User Acceptance Testing (UAT)** is the final validation phase where
actual business users verify that the system solves their real business
needs.

The main question of UAT:

\"Did we build the right solution for the business?\"

Many teams confuse:

**Functional Testing**

Question:

\"Does the system work correctly?\"

Performed by:

-   QA testers

-   Developers

Example:

\"Does the upload button work?\"

**User Acceptance Testing**

Question:

\"Does this solution help users complete their work?\"

Performed by:

-   Business users

-   Product Owner

-   Subject Matter Experts (SMEs)

Example:

\"Can a Pharmacovigilance specialist process a case successfully?\"

**2. Where UAT Fits in Agile**

Traditional flow:

Requirements

**Lesson 11: Business Process Modeling (BPM) for Agile Business

**1. What is Business Process Modeling?**

**Business Process Modeling (BPM)** is the technique of visually
representing how a business process works.

A BA uses process modeling to understand:

-   How work is currently performed

-   Who performs each activity

-   Where problems occur

-   How the process can be improved

Simple definition:

Business Process Modeling is creating a visual map of business
activities, decisions, people, and systems involved in completing a
business task.

**2. Why Does an Agile BA Need Process Modeling?**

Many requirements fail because teams understand **features** but not the
**business process**.

Example:

Business request:

\"Build an automated case management system.\"

A developer may ask:

-   What screens are required?

-   What buttons are needed?

But BA asks:

-   How is a case created today?

-   Who reviews it?

-   Who approves it?

-   What happens if information is missing?

-   What regulatory steps exist?

Process modeling helps discover the real requirement.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**2. Types of Stakeholder Conflicts**

**A. Requirement Conflict**

Two stakeholders want different things.

Automation vs Safety.

-   Regulatory requirement

-   Risk level

-   Business impact

**B. Priority Conflict**

Example:

Technical improvements

-   Business value

-   Risk

-   Compliance impact

-   User impact

**C. Scope Conflict**

Stakeholder asks for more features.

Example:

Original requirement:

\"AI extracts adverse events.\"

New request:

\"Also add voice assistant, mobile app, and predictive analytics.\"

Scope increases.

BA response:

Analyze:

-   Value

-   Effort

-   Timeline

-   Dependencies

Business wants:

\"AI should identify every adverse event with 100% accuracy.\"

-   Define acceptable accuracy

-   Add human validation

-   Improve model gradually

**Lesson 13: Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs) for Agile Business
Analysts**

**1. What are Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs)?**

A **Functional Requirement** explains:

\"What should the system do?\"

A **Non-Functional Requirement (NFR)** explains:

\"How well should the system perform?\"

**Example**

Functional Requirement:

\"The system should allow users to upload adverse event reports.\"

Non-Functional Requirements:

-   Upload should complete within 5 seconds

-   Only authorized users can upload

-   System should support 10,000 users

-   Data should be encrypted

**2. Why Are NFRs Important for Agile BAs?**

Many Agile teams focus heavily on features but ignore quality
attributes.

A feature may work but still fail in real life.

Example:

AI Pharmacovigilance Platform:

Functional requirement:

\"Generate safety reports.\"

System can generate reports.

But:

-   Takes 30 minutes to load ❌

-   Patient data is exposed ❌

-   Cannot handle peak workload ❌

The product fails.

A BA ensures quality expectations are defined early.

**3. Functional vs Non-Functional Requirements**

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Functional Requirement**       **Non-Functional Requirement**
  -------------------------------- --------------------------------------
  What system does                 How system performs

  Features                         Quality attributes

  User actions                     System behavior

  Business capability              Technical constraints
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Example:

**Functional:**

\"User can search cases.\"

**Non-Functional:**

\"Search results should appear within 3 seconds.\"

**4. Types of Non-Functional Requirements**

**A. Performance Requirements**

Defines system speed and response time.

Questions BA asks:

-   How fast should the system respond?

-   How many users will use it?

-   How much data is processed?

Example:

AI Case Processing:

Requirement:

\"AI extraction should complete within 60 seconds for a standard
document.\"

Other examples:

-   Dashboard loads within 3 seconds

-   Search results appear within 2 seconds

-   Report generation completes within 30 seconds

**B. Scalability Requirements**

Defines ability to handle growth.

Example:

Today:

10,000 cases/month

Future:

100,000 cases/month

Requirement:

\"The system should support increasing case volume without significant
performance degradation.\"

BA considers:

-   User growth

-   Data growth

-   Transaction growth

**C. Availability Requirements**

Defines system uptime.

Example:

A healthcare system may require:

99.9% availability.

Meaning:

System should rarely be unavailable.

BA questions:

-   Is the system business critical?

-   What happens if unavailable?

-   How quickly should service recover?

**D. Reliability Requirements**

Defines consistent operation.

Example:

Requirement:

\"The system should process cases accurately without data loss.\"

Important for:

-   Patient data

-   Regulatory submissions

-   Safety reports

**E. Security Requirements**

Extremely important in healthcare.

Security requirements include:

**Authentication**

Who can access?

Example:

Username + password + MFA

**Authorization**

What can users do?

Example:

Safety Associate:

Create cases

Medical Reviewer:

Approve cases

**Data Protection**

Example:

Patient information must be encrypted.

**Audit Trail**

System must record:

-   User

-   Action

-   Date/time

-   Changes made

**F. Usability Requirements**

Defines ease of use.

Example:

Requirement:

\"A new safety associate should be able to create a case without
extensive training.\"

BA considers:

-   User experience

-   Number of steps

-   Error messages

-   Navigation

**G. Compatibility Requirements**

Defines system integration ability.

Example:

AI PV platform must integrate with:

-   Drug databases

-   Regulatory systems

-   Existing company systems

**H. Maintainability Requirements**

Defines how easy the system is to support.

Example:

-   Clear documentation

-   Modular design

-   Easy updates

**5. BA Role in Capturing NFRs**

A BA does not define technical solutions.

A BA captures business expectations.

Example:

Business says:

\"We need a secure system.\"

BA converts into:

Requirement:

\"Only authorized users should access confidential safety information.\"

Technical team decides:

-   Encryption method

-   Authentication technology

**6. How to Discover NFRs**

Many stakeholders forget NFRs.

BA should ask specific questions.

**Performance Questions**

-   How many users will access the system?

-   How quickly should response happen?

-   How much data is processed daily?

**Security Questions**

-   Is data sensitive?

-   Who can access it?

-   What audit requirements exist?

**Availability Questions**

-   How critical is this system?

-   What downtime is acceptable?

**Compliance Questions**

-   Are there regulatory requirements?

-   Are audit records required?

**7. Writing Good NFRs**

Avoid vague statements.

Bad:

\"The system should be fast.\"

Good:

\"The system should display search results within 3 seconds for normal
user requests.\"

Bad:

\"The system should be secure.\"

Good:

\"The system should restrict case access based on user role
permissions.\"

**8. NFRs in Healthcare AI Products**

Let\'s apply this to AI Pharmacovigilance.

**Requirement:**

AI should extract adverse events from reports.

Functional Requirements:

✅ Extract event information\
✅ Identify suspect drug\
✅ Generate summary

Non-Functional Requirements:

**Accuracy**

\"AI extraction accuracy should meet defined acceptance threshold.\"

**Performance**

\"Processing should complete within 60 seconds.\"

**Security**

\"Patient information must be protected.\"

**Auditability**

\"AI-generated suggestions must be traceable.\"

**Availability**

\"System should be available during business operations.\"

**9. AI-Specific Non-Functional Requirements**

AI products have additional considerations.

**A. Model Accuracy**

Question:

How accurate should AI output be?

Example:

Drug extraction accuracy \>95%

**B. Explainability**

Important in healthcare.

Question:

Why did AI make this suggestion?

Example:

AI suggests:

\"Drug X may be related to event Y.\"

System should show:

Evidence from source text.

**C. Human Oversight**

AI should not always replace humans.

Requirement:

\"Critical safety decisions require human validation.\"

**D. Data Quality**

AI depends on data.

Requirement:

\"Training and operational data should meet quality standards.\"

**10. Creating NFR Acceptance Criteria**

Example:

User Story:

As a Medical Reviewer,\
I want AI-generated summaries,\
so that I can review cases faster.

NFR Acceptance Criteria:

**Performance**

Given:

A completed case exists

When:

User requests summary

Then:

Summary should generate within 30 seconds.

**Security**

Given:

User does not have reviewer permission

When:

They request summary

Then:

Access should be denied.

**Audit**

Given:

AI generates summary

When:

Summary is saved

Then:

System should record AI version and timestamp.

**11. BA Checklist for NFRs**

Before development:

✅ Performance expectations defined\
✅ Security requirements captured\
✅ User roles defined\
✅ Compliance requirements identified\
✅ Data requirements documented\
✅ Scalability considered\
✅ AI limitations understood
