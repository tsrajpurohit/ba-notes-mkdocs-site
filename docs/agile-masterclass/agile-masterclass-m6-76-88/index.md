**MODULE 6 -- Agile Metrics**

**Lesson 76: Why Agile Metrics Matter (Foundation)**

**Role Perspective:**\
As a future **Business Analyst / Product Analyst / Product Manager / AI
Product Manager**, understanding metrics is critical because Agile is
not only about \"delivering faster\"; it is about **delivering the right
value and continuously improving.**

**1. Concept: What are Agile Metrics?**

**Simple Definition**

**Agile Metrics are measurements used by Agile teams to understand
progress, performance, quality, flow, and improvement.**

They help answer questions like:

-   Are we delivering value?

-   Are we improving?

-   Are we predictable?

-   Are we building quality products?

-   Are customers happy?

Example:

A Pharmacovigilance team is developing an:

**AI Adverse Event Detection Platform**

Management asks:

\"Are we progressing well?\"

A weak answer:

\"We completed 50 user stories.\"

A better Agile answer:

-   Average cycle time reduced from 10 days to 5 days

-   Case processing accuracy increased from 85% to 95%

-   Release predictability improved from 60% to 90%

-   User satisfaction increased

These are metrics.

**2. Why Agile Metrics Exist**

**The Problem**

Without metrics, teams rely on opinions.

Example:

Product Owner:

\"The team is slow.\"

Developer:

\"We are working very hard.\"

Manager:

\"Why is release delayed?\"

Everyone has a different opinion.

Metrics create a common reality.

**Metrics help answer:**

**1. Where are we?**

Example:

How much work is completed?

**2. Where are we going?**

Example:

Can we release the product by December?

**3. What is blocking us?**

Example:

Why are user stories waiting 15 days?

**4. How can we improve?**

Example:

Why are defects increasing every sprint?

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine you are driving a car.

Without metrics:

You don\'t know:

-   Speed

-   Fuel level

-   Engine temperature

You may reach the destination, or you may fail.

Dashboard gives information:

Speed 80 km/hr

Fuel 50%

Temperature Normal

Distance 100 km

Agile metrics are the dashboard of a product team.

**5. Metrics vs KPIs**

Many people confuse these.

**Metric**

Any measurement.

Example:

Number of user stories completed.

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)**

A metric directly linked to business goals.

Example:

Goal:

Reduce drug safety case processing time.

KPI:

Average processing time reduced by 40%.

Comparison:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Metric**                **KPI**
  ------------------------- ---------------------------------------------
  Measures something        Measures important business outcome

  Many metrics exist        Few important KPIs

  Team-focused              Business-focused
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**13. Interview Questions**

**Q1. Why are Agile metrics important?**

Answer:

Agile metrics provide visibility into team performance, product
delivery, quality, and improvement opportunities. They help teams make
data-driven decisions.

**Q2. Should velocity be used to compare teams?**

Answer:

No. Velocity is a team-specific planning metric. Comparing velocity
between teams creates unhealthy competition.

**Q3. What is the difference between metric and KPI?**

Answer:

A metric measures any activity, while a KPI measures progress toward an
important business objective.

**Lesson 77: Velocity (Advanced Understanding)**

**Role Perspective:**\
As a **Business Analyst / Product Analyst / Product Manager**, velocity
helps you understand **how much work a team can realistically deliver**,
forecast releases, and identify improvement opportunities.

A common interview mistake is saying:

\"Velocity measures developer productivity.\"

This is incorrect.

Velocity measures **team delivery capacity**, not individual
performance.

**1. Concept: What is Velocity?**

**Simple Definition**

**Velocity is the amount of work a Scrum team completes during a Sprint,
usually measured in Story Points.**

Formula:

Velocity = Total Story Points Completed in a Sprint

Example:

Sprint duration:

2 weeks

Team committed:

40 Story Points

Completed:

35 Story Points

Velocity:

35 Story Points

**2. Why Velocity Exists**

**The Problem**

A Product Owner asks:

\"When can we release our AI Pharmacovigilance Platform?\"

The team says:

\"We don\'t know.\"

Velocity helps create a forecast.

Example:

Product Backlog:

300 Story Points

Team velocity:

30 points/sprint

Forecast:

300 ÷ 30 = 10 Sprints

If one Sprint = 2 weeks:

10 × 2 = 20 weeks

Approximate release:

5 months

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine you run a restaurant.

Every day you prepare meals.

Day 1:

100 meals

Day 2:

120 meals

Day 3:

110 meals

Your average capacity:

≈110 meals/day

You can predict:

\"How many meals can we prepare next week?\"

Velocity works similarly.

**4. Technical Explanation**

Velocity uses:

**Story Points**

Not:

❌ Hours\
❌ Lines of code\
❌ Number of tasks

Example:

Sprint 1:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **User Story**                                   **Points**
  ------------------------------------------------ ----------------------
  Login                                            3

  Patient Profile                                  5

  Case Upload                                      8

  Dashboard                                        13
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Completed:

3 + 5 + 8 + 13

= 29 Story Points

Velocity:

29

**5. How Velocity is Calculated**

**Example: AI Pharmacovigilance Platform**

**Sprint 1**

Completed:

-   User Authentication → 5

-   PDF Upload → 8

-   Case Database → 13

Velocity:

5 + 8 + 13 = 26

**Sprint 2**

Completed:

-   MedDRA Search → 8

-   Case Validation → 5

-   Reporting Module → 13

Velocity:

8 + 5 + 13 = 26

**Sprint 3**

Completed:

-   Signal Detection → 13

-   Dashboard → 8

Velocity:

21

Average Velocity:

(26 + 26 + 21) ÷ 3

= 24.3

≈ 24 Story Points/Sprint

**11. Velocity vs Productivity**

This is a common interview trap.

Wrong:

\"Velocity shows how productive developers are.\"

Correct:

\"Velocity shows how much value a Scrum team can deliver based on
completed work.\"

Why?

A team can increase velocity by:

-   Breaking stories smaller

-   Changing estimation

-   Taking easier work

But business value may not increase.

**12. Common Velocity Mistakes**

**Mistake 1:**

Comparing team velocities

Wrong:

Team A velocity = 50

Team B velocity = 30

Team A is better.

Why wrong?

Different teams estimate differently.

**Mistake 2:**

Forcing higher velocity

Example:

Manager:

\"Last sprint you delivered 30 points. Now deliver 50.\"

Result:

-   Poor quality

-   Artificial estimates

-   Burnout

**Mistake 3:**

Changing Story Point values

Example:

Login was 5 points.

After completion:

\"Make it 3 points.\"

This destroys historical data.

✅ Use for forecasting\
✅ Look for trends\
✅ Discuss changes in retrospectives

❌ Measure individuals\
❌ Compare teams\
❌ Set targets

**15. Interview Questions**

**Q1. What is velocity?**

Answer:

Velocity is the amount of story points a Scrum team completes in a
sprint. It helps teams forecast future delivery.

**Q2. Can velocity be compared between teams?**

Answer:

No. Velocity is relative to each team\'s estimation approach and should
only be used within the same team.

**Q3. What happens if velocity suddenly drops?**

Good answer:

The team should investigate causes such as dependencies, technical
issues, scope changes, team availability, or increased complexity.

**Q4. Should Product Owners maximize velocity?**

Answer:

No. Product Owners should maximize business value. Velocity is only a
planning tool.

**Lesson 78: Cycle Time (Deep Dive)**

**Role Perspective:** Business Analyst • Product Analyst • Product

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

-   Understand what Cycle Time is.

-   Differentiate Cycle Time from Lead Time (a very common interview
    topic).

-   Identify bottlenecks using Cycle Time.

-   Interpret Cycle Time reports in Jira.

-   Apply Cycle Time to Healthcare, Pharmacovigilance, and AI products.

**1. Concept**

**What is Cycle Time?**

**Simple Definition**

**Cycle Time is the time it takes to complete work after the team starts
working on it.**

It answers the question:

**\"Once we start working on something, how long does it take until it
is finished?\"**

**2. Why Does Cycle Time Exist?**

Imagine you\'re a Product Manager.

A stakeholder asks:

\"Once the development team starts working on the AI Duplicate Detection
feature, how long will it take to finish?\"

Velocity won\'t answer that because Velocity measures **how much** work
is completed in a Sprint.

Cycle Time answers **how long** a single work item takes.

Without Cycle Time:

-   Delivery becomes unpredictable.

-   Stakeholders lose confidence.

-   Bottlenecks remain hidden.

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine ordering a pizza.

Timeline:

6:00 PM Order placed

6:20 PM Chef starts making pizza

6:45 PM Pizza ready

7:00 PM Delivered

Cycle Time begins when the chef starts cooking.

6:20 PM → 6:45 PM

Cycle Time = 25 minutes

Notice that waiting before cooking **is not part of Cycle Time**.

**4. Technical Explanation**

Typical Agile workflow:

Backlog

│

▼

To Do

│

▼

In Progress

│

▼

Code Review

│

▼

Testing

│

▼

Done

Cycle Time begins when the work item enters:

**In Progress**

Cycle Time ends when it reaches:

**Done**

Formula:

Cycle Time = Done Date − Start Date

**13. What Increases Cycle Time?**

Common causes:

**Unclear Requirements**

Developers repeatedly ask questions.

**Too Many Approvals**

Waiting for:

-   Manager

-   Security

-   Legal

-   Compliance

**External Dependencies**

Example:

Waiting for another team to deliver an API.

**Large User Stories**

Example:

\"Build complete AI module\"

Instead of:

-   Upload PDF

-   Extract patient

-   Detect duplicate

-   Generate report

Large stories naturally take longer.

**Excessive Work in Progress (WIP)**

Developers start many stories but finish few.

Result:

Cycle Time increases.

**14. How to Reduce Cycle Time**

Good Agile teams:

✅ Split large stories.

✅ Limit WIP.

✅ Clarify requirements early.

✅ Automate testing.

✅ Remove approval bottlenecks.

✅ Improve CI/CD.

✅ Encourage collaboration.

**19. Interview Questions**

**Q1. What is Cycle Time?**

**Answer:**

Cycle Time measures the time taken to complete a work item after
development begins until it is finished.

**Q2. Why is Cycle Time important?**

**Answer:**

It improves predictability, identifies bottlenecks, and helps teams
deliver work more efficiently.

**Q3. Can a low Velocity still have a good Cycle Time?**

**Answer:**

Yes. A team may complete fewer but larger stories quickly, or be
intentionally working on high-complexity items. Velocity and Cycle Time
measure different aspects of delivery.

**Q4. How can a BA reduce Cycle Time?**

**Answer:**

By providing clear requirements, defining acceptance criteria, resolving
stakeholder questions quickly, and reducing ambiguity before development
starts.

**Lesson 80: Flow Efficiency (Deep Dive)**

**Role Perspective:** Business Analyst • Product Analyst • Product

**Flow Efficiency is one of the most powerful---but least
understood---Agile metrics.**

Elite organizations like Amazon, Microsoft, and Spotify focus not just
on working faster, but on **reducing waiting time**. This is where Flow
Efficiency becomes valuable.

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

-   Calculate Flow Efficiency.

-   Apply Flow Efficiency to Healthcare, Pharmacovigilance, and AI
    products.

-   Interpret Flow Efficiency alongside Lead Time and Cycle Time.

**1. Concept**

**What is Flow Efficiency?**

**Simple Definition**

**Flow Efficiency measures how much of the total time a work item is
actually being worked on versus waiting.**

It answers:

**\"How much of the elapsed time was productive work?\"**

**2. Why Does Flow Efficiency Exist?**

Imagine a user story takes **10 days** to finish.

You might think:

\"It took 10 days of work.\"

But when you investigate:

-   Developers worked only **3 days**.

-   The story spent **7 days waiting**:

    -   Waiting for approvals.

    -   Waiting for testing.

    -   Waiting for another team.

    -   Waiting for deployment.

Flow Efficiency reveals this hidden waste.

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Suppose you visit a hospital for a blood test.

Timeline:

9:00 AM Arrive

9:20 AM Registration

9:50 AM Waiting

10:10 AM Blood Sample (5 minutes)

10:15 AM Waiting

11:00 AM Doctor Review (10 minutes)

11:10 AM Leave

Total Time:

2 hours 10 minutes (130 minutes)

Actual medical work:

-   Blood sample = 5 minutes

-   Doctor review = 10 minutes

Total active work:

15 minutes

Most of your visit was spent waiting.

This is exactly what Flow Efficiency measures.

**4. Technical Explanation**

Formula:

Flow Efficiency = (Active Working Time ÷ Total Elapsed Time) × 100%

Example:

User Story:

Total elapsed time:

10 days

Actual work:

4 days

Calculation:

(4 ÷ 10) × 100

= 40%

Flow Efficiency:

**40%**

Meaning:

The work item spent **60% of its time waiting**.

**Lesson 81: Escaped Defects (Deep Dive)**

**Role Perspective:** Business Analyst • Product Analyst • Product

**If Velocity tells you how fast you\'re delivering, Escaped Defects
tell you whether you\'re delivering quality.**

A team can have excellent Velocity and terrible quality. High-performing
Agile teams optimize **both speed and quality**.

**Learning Objectives**

**1. Concept**

**What is an Escaped Defect?**

**Simple Definition**

An **Escaped Defect** is a bug that was **not found during development
or testing** and reaches the customer or production environment.

In simple words:

**A defect that \"escaped\" your quality process.**

**2. Why Does This Metric Exist?**

Imagine your team says:

\"We delivered every Sprint on time.\"

Great!

But one week later:

Customers complain:

-   Login doesn\'t work.

-   AI gives incorrect answers.

-   Reports are missing data.

The team delivered **quickly**, but **poor quality**.

Escaped Defects help answer:

**\"How many problems reached our users?\"**

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine a pharmaceutical company manufactures tablets.

Quality checks happen before shipping.

If damaged tablets reach pharmacies:

The quality process failed.

The damaged tablets are like **Escaped Defects**.

**4. Technical Explanation**

Typical software flow:

Requirements

│

▼

Development

│

▼

Code Review

│

▼

Testing

│

▼

Release

│

▼

Customer

If a bug is found:

-   During Development ✅

-   During Testing ✅

It is **not** an escaped defect.

If the customer finds it after release ❌

It **is** an Escaped Defect.

Formula:

Example:

8

8 ÷ 100 × 100

**8%**

**1. Concept**

**What is Sprint Predictability?**

**Simple Definition**

**Sprint Predictability measures how consistently a Scrum Team delivers
the work it committed to during Sprint Planning.**

It answers:

**\"Did we deliver what we said we would?\"**

**2. Why Does Sprint Predictability Exist?**

Imagine you are a Product Manager.

You tell the CEO:

\"The AI Duplicate Detection feature will be ready this Sprint.\"

Sprint ends.

Feature is only 50% complete.

Now:

-   Customers wait.

-   Release plans change.

-   Stakeholders lose confidence.

The issue isn\'t just that work is unfinished---it\'s that **the
forecast was unreliable**.

Sprint Predictability helps improve trust.

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine a courier company.

Every day they promise:

\"We will deliver 100 packages.\"

Scenario A:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Day**                    **Promised**          **Delivered**
  -------------------------- --------------------- ----------------------
  Monday                     100                   99

  Tuesday                    100                   101

  Wednesday                  100                   98
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Very predictable.

Scenario B:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Day**                    **Promised**          **Delivered**
  -------------------------- --------------------- ----------------------
  Monday                     100                   45

  Tuesday                    100                   140

  Wednesday                  100                   70
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Average deliveries may still be close to 100, but customers cannot rely
on them.

Consistency matters.

**4. Technical Explanation**

(Completed Story Points ÷ Committed Story Points) × 100

Committed:

40 Story Points

36 Story Points

Calculation:

36 ÷ 40 × 100

= 90%

Sprint Predictability:

**90%**

**Role Perspective:** Business Analyst • Product Manager • Scrum Master
• Engineering Manager • Agile Coach

**If the Burndown Chart tells you \"Are we finishing work?\", the
Cumulative Flow Diagram tells you \"How is work flowing through the

Companies like Amazon, Microsoft, Spotify, Atlassian, and many large

**1. Concept**

**What is a Cumulative Flow Diagram?**

**Simple Definition**

A **Cumulative Flow Diagram (CFD)** is a chart that shows **how work
moves through different stages of your workflow over time.**

Unlike a Burndown Chart, which focuses on *remaining work*, a CFD shows:

-   How much work is waiting.

-   How much work is in progress.

-   How much work is completed.

-   Whether work is flowing smoothly.

**2. Why Does CFD Exist?**

Imagine your Product Manager says:

\"We completed only 15 stories this Sprint.\"

The next question is:

**\"Why?\"**

Possible reasons:

-   Too much work waiting.

-   Testing is overloaded.

-   Code reviews are delayed.

-   Developers started too many tasks.

A CFD helps answer these questions visually.

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine a hospital emergency department.

Patients move through stages:

Registration

↓

Doctor Assessment

↓

Lab Tests

↓

Treatment

↓

Discharge

If many patients are waiting for lab tests, the queue grows.

This indicates a bottleneck in the lab---not that doctors are working
slowly.

A CFD works exactly the same way for software delivery.

**4. Technical Explanation**

A CFD tracks the number of work items in each workflow state.

Example workflow:

To Do

↓

In Progress

↓

Code Review

↓

Testing

↓

Done

Each stage is represented by a colored band (or layer) on the chart.

The height of each band shows **how many items are in that stage**.

**Lesson 86: Throughput (Masterclass)**

**Role Perspective:** Business Analyst • Product Manager • Scrum Master
• Agile Coach • Engineering Manager

**Throughput answers one simple but powerful question:**

**\"How many work items does the team complete in a given period?\"**

Unlike Velocity, Throughput doesn\'t care about Story Points. It focuses
on **finished work**, making it especially useful in **Kanban, Lean,
DevOps, and AI product teams**.

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

-   Understand what Throughput is.

-   Calculate Throughput.

-   Differentiate Throughput from Velocity.

-   Use Throughput for forecasting.

-   Understand its role in Scrum and Kanban.

-   Learn the basics of Monte Carlo Forecasting.

-   Apply Throughput to Healthcare and AI projects.

-   Answer common interview questions.

**1. Concept**

**What is Throughput?**

**Simple Definition**

**Throughput is the number of work items completed in a specific period
of time.**

It answers:

**\"How many items did we finish?\"**

Notice that Throughput measures **count**, not **size**.

**2. Why Does Throughput Exist?**

Imagine two Scrum teams.

**Team A**

Completed:

-   8 User Stories

**Team B**

Completed:

-   16 User Stories

At first glance, Team B seems faster.

But what if:

-   Team A completed eight **large** stories.

-   Team B completed sixteen **very small** stories.

Throughput only tells you **how many items were completed**, not how
complex they were.

That is why Throughput and Velocity are complementary metrics.

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine a pharmacy.

During one day:

-   150 prescriptions are filled.

The pharmacist doesn\'t measure:

-   How complex each prescription was.

They simply measure:

**How many prescriptions were completed today?**

That is Throughput.

**4. Technical Explanation**

Formula:

Throughput = Number of Completed Work Items

Time period examples:

-   Per day

-   Per week

-   Per Sprint

-   Per month

**Example**

Sprint:

2 weeks

Completed:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Work Item**                                  **Status**
  ---------------------------------------------- ------------------------
  Login                                          Done

  Dashboard                                      Done

  Reports                                        Done

  PDF Upload                                     Done

  AI Extraction                                  Done
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Throughput:

**5 work items**

**5. Throughput vs Velocity**

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Throughput**                     **Velocity**
  ---------------------------------- ------------------------------------
  Counts completed items             Counts Story Points completed

  Doesn\'t consider complexity       Reflects estimated complexity

  Common in Kanban                   Common in Scrum

  Easier to calculate                Requires estimation

  Useful for flow                    Useful for Sprint planning
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Example**

Sprint A:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Story**                         **Story Points**
  --------------------------------- -------------------------------------
  Login                             3

  Dashboard                         8

  Reports                           5
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Throughput:

**3 stories**

Velocity:

**16 Story Points**

Both metrics describe the same Sprint from different perspectives.

-   Patient Registration

-   Appointment Booking

-   Billing

-   Lab Results

-   Doctor Schedule

Throughput:

**5 features**

Even if one feature was much larger than another, Throughput simply
counts completed items.

**9. Throughput Trend**

Suppose a team\'s Throughput over six Sprints is:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Sprint**               **Throughput**
  ------------------------ ----------------------------------------------
  1                        5

  2                        6

  3                        5

  4                        7

  5                        6

  6                        5
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Average Throughput:

(5 + 6 + 5 + 7 + 6 + 5) ÷ 6

= 5.67

You might forecast that the team will complete **around 5--6 items per
Sprint**, assuming work items are of similar size.

**10. Throughput and Forecasting**

Suppose your backlog contains:

40 user stories.

Average Throughput:

5 stories per Sprint.

Estimated number of Sprints:

40 ÷ 5 = 8 Sprints

Remember:

This is an estimate, not a guarantee.

Changes in scope, team capacity, or story size can affect the outcome.

**11. Introduction to Monte Carlo Forecasting**

Many modern Agile tools use **Monte Carlo Simulation** for forecasting.

Instead of asking:

\"Exactly when will we finish?\"

It asks:

\"Based on our historical delivery, what is the most likely completion
window?\"

**Simple Analogy**

Imagine tossing a coin thousands of times.

You can\'t predict the next toss with certainty.

But you can estimate probabilities.

Monte Carlo uses the same idea with historical delivery data.

**Example**

Historical Throughput:

-   Sprint 1 = 5

-   Sprint 2 = 6

-   Sprint 3 = 4

-   Sprint 4 = 7

-   Sprint 5 = 5

Instead of saying:

\"The release will finish in exactly 8 Sprints.\"

Monte Carlo may estimate:

-   **70% probability:** Finished within 8 Sprints.

-   **90% probability:** Finished within 9 Sprints.

This helps Product Managers communicate uncertainty more honestly.

**12. Scrum vs Kanban**

**Scrum**

Primary metric:

Velocity

Secondary metric:

Throughput

**Kanban**

Primary metric:

Throughput

Because Kanban teams often do not estimate Story Points, Throughput
becomes one of the main measures of delivery.

**13. Jira Example**

Jira dashboards can show:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Sprint**             **Completed Issues**
  ---------------------- ------------------------------------------------
  Sprint 1               12

  Sprint 2               14

  Sprint 3               13

  Sprint 4               15
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

A Scrum Master can identify trends:

-   Stable Throughput

-   Increasing Throughput

-   Declining Throughput

The focus should be on understanding **why**, not simply expecting
higher numbers every Sprint.

**14. Common Mistakes**

**Mistake 1**

Comparing Throughput between different teams.

Different teams:

-   Build different products.

-   Handle different complexity.

-   Define work items differently.

Direct comparisons are often misleading.

**Mistake 2**

Ignoring work item size.

Completing:

-   10 tiny tasks

is not necessarily better than completing:

-   3 large, high-value features.

**Mistake 3**

Chasing higher Throughput.

Teams may split work into many tiny tickets just to increase the metric.

This creates misleading data.

**Mistake 4**

Using Throughput to evaluate individuals.

Throughput is a **team metric**, not a personal performance measure.

**15. Best Practices**

✔ Track Throughput over multiple Sprints.

✔ Combine Throughput with Lead Time and Cycle Time.

✔ Keep work items reasonably consistent in size.

✔ Use Throughput for forecasting, not competition.

✔ Discuss unusual changes during Retrospectives.

**Lesson 87: Work in Progress (WIP) (Masterclass)**

**Role Perspective:** Business Analyst • Product Manager • Scrum Master
• Agile Coach • Engineering Manager

**One of the biggest mistakes Agile teams make is starting too much work
instead of finishing work.**

High-performing companies like Amazon, Microsoft, Spotify, Toyota, and
many healthcare organizations focus on **limiting Work in Progress
(WIP)** because it improves flow, quality, and predictability.

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

-   Understand what Work in Progress (WIP) is.

-   Learn why limiting WIP improves delivery.

-   Understand Little\'s Law (in simple terms).

-   Use WIP limits in Kanban and Scrum.

-   Relate WIP to Lead Time, Cycle Time, Throughput, and Flow
    Efficiency.

-   Apply WIP concepts to Healthcare, Pharmacovigilance, and AI
    products.

-   Answer common interview questions.

**1. Concept**

**What is Work in Progress (WIP)?**

**Simple Definition**

**Work in Progress (WIP)** is the amount of work that has been **started
but not yet completed**.

Think of WIP as **unfinished work**.

**2. Why Does WIP Exist?**

Imagine a developer starts:

-   Login

-   Dashboard

-   Reports

-   Notifications

-   AI Module

All at the same time.

After one week:

Nothing is finished.

Customers receive **zero value**.

Now imagine another developer:

-   Starts Login.

-   Finishes Login.

-   Starts Dashboard.

-   Finishes Dashboard.

Customers begin receiving usable features much sooner.

This is why Agile emphasizes **finishing before starting more work**.

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine you\'re cooking dinner.

Scenario A:

You start:

-   Rice

-   Curry

-   Chapati

-   Dessert

-   Soup

Everything is half done.

Dinner isn\'t ready.

Scenario B:

You prepare:

1.  Rice ✔

2.  Curry ✔

3.  Chapati ✔

4.  Soup ✔

5.  Dessert ✔

The meal is ready sooner, with less confusion.

The second approach keeps WIP low.

**4. Technical Explanation**

**12. Little\'s Law (Simple Explanation)**

Little\'s Law is a foundational concept in Lean and Kanban.

**Formula**

Lead Time = WIP ÷ Throughput

Where:

-   **Lead Time** = Time from request to completion.

-   **WIP** = Number of items in progress.

-   **Throughput** = Number of items completed per unit of time.

**Example**

Suppose:

-   WIP = 20 stories

-   Throughput = 5 stories per week

Lead Time:

20 ÷ 5 = 4 weeks

Now reduce WIP:

-   WIP = 10

-   Throughput = 5

Lead Time:

10 ÷ 5 = 2 weeks

Reducing WIP often reduces Lead Time.

**Note:** Little\'s Law assumes a relatively stable system with
consistent arrival and completion rates. It\'s a useful guide, not an
exact prediction for every project.

**16. Common Mistakes**

**Mistake 1**

Believing busy people mean productive teams.

Reality:

Many active tasks can hide slow delivery.

**Mistake 2**

Starting new work whenever someone becomes free.

Sometimes it\'s better to help finish existing work.

**Mistake 3**

Ignoring bottlenecks.

If Testing is overloaded, adding more work to Development usually makes
the problem worse.

**Mistake 4**

Using WIP limits as rigid rules.

WIP limits should encourage healthy flow, not prevent urgent work when
exceptions are justified.

**17. Best Practices**

✔ Finish work before starting new work.

✔ Set realistic WIP limits.

✔ Keep user stories small.

✔ Swarm on blocked work (have team members collaborate to finish it).

✔ Monitor bottlenecks regularly.

✔ Review WIP during Daily Scrums.
