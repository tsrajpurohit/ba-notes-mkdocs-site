**1. What is Confluence?**

**Simple Definition**

Confluence is a **collaborative documentation and knowledge management
platform** used by teams to create, store, organize, and share
information.

Think of it as:

A company\'s digital knowledge library.

**Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine a hospital.

Different departments maintain documents:

-   Medical procedures

-   Training manuals

-   SOPs

-   Research documents

-   Meeting notes

-   Policies

A physical library stores everything.

Confluence is the digital version of that library.

**Technical Definition**

Confluence is a collaboration tool developed by Atlassian.

It is commonly integrated with Jira.

**2. Why Does Confluence Exist?**

Before tools like Confluence, companies used:

-   Word documents

-   Email attachments

-   Shared folders

Problems:

❌ Multiple versions

❌ Lost documents

❌ Difficult collaboration

❌ No history tracking

❌ Poor knowledge sharing

Confluence solves this by providing:

✔ One central location

✔ Version history

✔ Comments

✔ Collaboration

✔ Search

✔ Templates

**1. What is Jira?**

**Simple Definition**

**Jira is a work management and issue-tracking tool used to plan, track,
and deliver software projects.**

Think of Jira as the **central workspace** where every requirement,
task, bug, sprint, and release is managed.

**2. Why Was Jira Created?**

Imagine building a Hospital Management System.

The project has:

-   300 requirements

-   1,500 user stories

-   20 developers

-   8 testers

-   3 Business Analysts

-   2 Product Managers

-   Hundreds of bugs

Managing this in Excel would quickly become unmanageable.

Jira was created to solve this problem.

**3. Real-Life Analogy**

Imagine you\'re building a new hospital.

You have:

-   Architects

-   Electricians

-   Plumbers

-   Engineers

-   Contractors

Everyone has different tasks.

Jira acts like the **master construction board**, showing:

-   What needs to be done.

-   Who is responsible.

-   What\'s in progress.

-   What\'s completed.

-   What\'s blocked.

Bad:

Using Jira as a personal to-do list.

Jira is designed for **team collaboration**, not just individual task
tracking.

**3. Company-managed vs Team-managed Projects**

This is one of the **most common Jira interview questions.**

**Company-managed Project**

Managed by Jira Administrators.

Best for:

-   Medium/Large companies.

-   Enterprise environments.

-   Multiple Agile teams.

-   Standardized workflows.

Characteristics:

✔ Common workflows

✔ Standard issue types

✔ Central permissions

✔ Governance

✔ Highly scalable

**Team-managed Project**

Managed by the project team itself.

Best for:

-   Small teams.

-   Startups.

-   Proof of Concepts.

-   MVPs.

Characteristics:

✔ Quick setup

✔ Flexible

✔ Team controls configuration

✔ Minimal administration

**Comparison**

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Company-managed**                        **Team-managed**
  ------------------------------------------ ----------------------------
  Enterprise                                 Small teams

  Central administration                     Team controlled

  Complex workflows                          Simple workflows

  Highly customizable                        Easier to manage

  Best for large organizations               Best for startups
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Which Should You Learn?**

For interviews:

Know **both**.

For enterprise healthcare companies:

Expect **Company-managed** projects.

**4. Scrum Project vs Kanban Project**

When creating a Jira project, you often choose a template.

**Scrum Project**

Best when work is delivered in **Sprints**.

Contains:

-   Product Backlog

-   Sprint Backlog

-   Sprint Planning

-   Burndown Charts

-   Velocity Reports

Example:

AI Pharmacovigilance Platform

2-week Sprint

Sprint Goal:

AI Duplicate Detection

**Kanban Project**

Best for continuous work.

No Sprint Planning.

Focuses on:

-   Flow

-   WIP limits

-   Continuous delivery

Example:

Customer Support Team

New issues arrive daily and are resolved continuously.

**Scrum vs Kanban**

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Scrum**                    **Kanban**
  ---------------------------- ------------------------------------------
  Sprint-based                 Continuous flow

  Sprint Planning              No Sprint Planning

  Sprint Review                Continuous review

  Velocity                     Throughput/Cycle Time

  Time-boxed                   Flow-based
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Creating too many Labels.

Giving everyone administrator access.

permissions they need.
