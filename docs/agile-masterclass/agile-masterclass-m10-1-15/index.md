# Agile Masterclass_M10 (1-15)

**MODULE 10 -- PRODUCT MANAGEMENT IN AGILE**

**Lesson 1: Product Management Fundamentals**

**1. What is Product Management?**

Product Management is the discipline of:

**Identifying customer problems, creating solutions, delivering value,
and continuously improving the product.**

A Product Manager (PM) acts as the connection between:

Customers

↓

Business Goals

↓

Product Strategy

↓

Development Team

↓

Product Release

↓

Customer Feedback

A Product Manager asks:

-   What problem should we solve?

-   Who are we solving it for?

-   Why is this important?

-   What should we build?

-   How will we measure success?

**2. What is a Product?**

A product is anything that provides value to users.

It can be:

**Physical Product**

Example:

-   Smartphone

-   Medical device

**Digital Product**

Example:

-   Mobile application

-   SaaS platform

-   Healthcare software

**AI Product**

Example:

AI Pharmacovigilance Platform:

A system that helps safety teams process adverse event cases faster
using AI.

**3. Product vs Project**

A common interview question.

**Project**

A temporary effort to deliver something.

Example:

\"Build a hospital management application in 6 months.\"

Focus:

-   Timeline

-   Budget

-   Scope

**Product**

A continuous journey of creating value.

Example:

\"Continuously improve hospital management software based on user
needs.\"

Focus:

-   Users

-   Value

-   Growth

-   Improvement

Comparison:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Project**                  **Product**
  ---------------------------- ------------------------------------------
  Temporary                    Continuous

  Has end date                 Evolves continuously

  Deliver output               Deliver value

  Success = completion         Success = user/business impact
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**4. Why Does Product Management Exist?**

Without Product Management:

Companies may build:

❌ Features nobody needs\
❌ Products without market demand\
❌ Solutions without clear goals

Product Management ensures:

✅ Build the right product\
✅ For the right users\
✅ At the right time\
✅ With measurable outcomes

**5. Role of a Product Manager**

A Product Manager is responsible for product success.

A PM does not usually:

❌ Write all code\
❌ Design every screen\
❌ Manage every task

Instead, PM:

✅ Defines direction\
✅ Understands users\
✅ Prioritizes problems\
✅ Makes product decisions\
✅ Aligns teams

**Lesson 2: Product Vision**

**1. What is Product Vision?**

A **Product Vision** describes the future state that a product wants to
create.

It answers:

**\"What future are we trying to build, and why does this product
matter?\"**

A product vision gives direction to:

-   Product team

-   Engineering team

-   Design team

-   Business stakeholders

-   Customers

Without a clear vision:

Teams may build many features but lose sight of the real purpose.

Example:

Bad vision:

\"Build an AI dashboard.\"

This describes a feature, not a vision.

Good vision:

\"Help pharmaceutical safety teams detect and manage drug risks faster
through intelligent AI-powered solutions.\"

This describes:

-   Who benefits

-   What problem is solved

-   What future we want

**2. Why Product Vision Matters**

A strong vision helps:

**1. Align Teams**

Everyone understands:

-   Why we are building

-   Who we are helping

-   What success looks like

**2. Make Better Decisions**

When a new feature request comes:

Question:

\"Does this move us closer to our vision?\"

If yes:

Consider building.

If no:

Avoid distraction.

**3. Prioritize Better**

Limited resources require choices.

Vision helps decide:

Feature A:

Improves patient safety.

Feature B:

Only improves visual design.

Vision helps choose A.

**3. Product Vision vs Product Mission**

These are often confused.

**Product Vision**

Focus:

**Future destination**

Question:

\"Where do we want to go?\"

Example:

\"Create a world where medication safety monitoring is proactive and
intelligent.\"

**Product Mission**

Focus:

**Current purpose**

Question:

\"What do we do every day to reach that future?\"

Example:

\"We help pharmaceutical companies analyze safety data using AI
automation.\"

Comparison:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Product Vision**                     **Product Mission**
  -------------------------------------- --------------------------------
  Future state                           Current purpose

  Long-term direction                    Daily activity

  Inspirational                          Practical
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**4. Product Vision vs Product Strategy**

Another common confusion.

**Vision**

Where?

Example:

\"Make drug safety monitoring intelligent.\"

**Strategy**

How?

Example:

\"Start with AI case processing, then expand into predictive safety
analytics.\"

**Roadmap**

When?

Example:

2026:

AI extraction

2027:

Signal detection

2028:

Predictive analytics

Relationship:

Vision

Where are we going?

↓

Strategy

How will we get there?

↓

Roadmap

When will we deliver?

**5. Elements of a Strong Product Vision**

A good product vision includes:

**1. Target User**

Who are we helping?

Example:

Pharmacovigilance professionals

**2. User Problem**

What problem exists?

Example:

Manual safety case processing is slow.

**3. Solution Direction**

What change will we create?

Example:

AI-assisted safety intelligence.

**4. Future Impact**

Why does it matter?

Example:

Faster detection of medicine risks.

**Product Vision Formula**

A simple framework:

For \[target users\]\
who struggle with \[problem\],\
our product provides \[solution\]\
so that \[desired impact\].

Example:

For pharmaceutical safety teams who struggle with manual case
processing, our AI platform provides intelligent automation so that
medicine risks can be identified faster and more accurately.

**Lesson 3: Product Strategy**

**1. What is Product Strategy?**

A **Product Strategy** defines how a company will achieve its product
vision.

It answers:

**\"How will we win in the market and create value for users and the
business?\"**

Product Vision:

\"Where do we want to go?\"

Product Strategy:

\"How will we get there?\"

Example:

**Vision:**

\"Transform drug safety monitoring using AI.\"

**Strategy:**

1.  Start with AI-assisted case processing

2.  Reduce manual workload

3.  Build trust with healthcare professionals

4.  Expand into predictive safety intelligence

**2. Why Product Strategy Matters**

Without strategy:

Teams may build:

❌ Too many features\
❌ Features without users\
❌ Products without differentiation

A strong strategy helps:

✅ Decide what to build\
✅ Decide what NOT to build\
✅ Allocate resources\
✅ Compete effectively\
✅ Measure progress

**10. Product-Market Fit (PMF)**

One of the most important product concepts.

Product-market fit means:

The product solves a real problem for a specific market, and users find
enough value to continue using it.

Signs of Product-Market Fit:

✅ Users actively use product\
✅ Customers recommend it\
✅ Demand increases\
✅ Retention is high

Example:

AI PV Platform achieves PMF when:

-   Safety teams depend on it daily

-   Processing time reduces significantly

-   Companies renew subscriptions

**Lesson 4: Market Research and Competitive Analysis**

**1. What is Market Research?**

**Market Research** is the process of collecting and analyzing
information about:

-   Customers

-   Industry

-   Competitors

-   Market trends

-   Business opportunities

A Product Manager uses market research to answer:

**\"Is this the right problem to solve, and is there a market that needs
our solution?\"**

Without research, companies may build:

❌ Products nobody wants\
❌ Features users do not need\
❌ Solutions that cannot compete

**2. Why Market Research is Important for Product Managers**

Market research helps PMs:

**1. Understand Customer Problems**

Example:

A pharma company says:

\"We need AI.\"

A PM investigates:

Actual problem:

\"Safety associates spend 70% of their time manually reviewing
reports.\"

The real opportunity is workflow automation.

**2. Identify Market Opportunities**

Example:

Observation:

-   Regulatory workload increasing

-   Safety data increasing

-   AI adoption growing

Opportunity:

AI-powered pharmacovigilance automation.

**3. Make Better Product Decisions**

Instead of:

\"I think users need this.\"

PM says:

\"Research shows 80% of users face this problem.\"

**3. Types of Market Research**

Market research is divided into two categories:

Primary Research

\+

Secondary Research

**4. Primary Research**

Primary research means collecting new information directly from users.

Sources:

-   Customer interviews

-   Surveys

-   User observation

-   Focus groups

-   Feedback sessions

Example:

Interview 20 safety associates.

Questions:

-   What tasks take most time?

-   What problems occur daily?

-   What tools do you currently use?

**Advantages**

✅ Real customer insights\
✅ Understand user pain points\
✅ Discover unmet needs

**Limitations**

❌ Time consuming\
❌ Small sample size

**5. Secondary Research**

Secondary research uses existing information.

Sources:

-   Industry reports

-   Research papers

-   Competitor websites

-   Market analysis

-   Regulatory publications

Example:

Researching:

-   Growth of AI in healthcare

-   Pharma technology trends

-   Regulatory technology adoption

**Advantages**

✅ Faster\
✅ Large amount of information

**Limitations**

❌ May not answer specific user problems

**6. Market Research Process**

A PM follows a structured process:

Define Research Goal

↓

Identify Target Users

↓

Collect Data

↓

Analyze Insights

↓

Identify Opportunities

↓

Make Product Decisions

**7. Defining Research Questions**

Good research starts with good questions.

Bad question:

\"Do you like AI?\"

Problem:

Too broad.

Better questions:

-   What tasks consume the most time?

-   What causes delays?

-   What tools are currently used?

-   What improvements would create the most value?

**9. Market Sizing**

A PM needs to understand market opportunity.

Three common concepts:

**TAM (Total Addressable Market)**

Total possible market.

Example:

All companies globally needing safety software.

**SAM (Serviceable Available Market)**

Market you can realistically serve.

Example:

Pharma companies in selected regions.

**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**

Realistic initial target.

Example:

First 100 pharma customers.

Relationship:

TAM

Largest opportunity

↓

SAM

Target market

↓

SOM

Initial achievable market

assumptions.

The problem was different.

✅ Build customer-focused products

**Lesson 7: MVP (Minimum Viable Product)**

**1. What is MVP?**

**MVP (Minimum Viable Product)** is the simplest version of a product
that:

1.  Solves a real user problem

2.  Provides enough value for early users

3.  Allows the team to learn from feedback

Simple definition:

**MVP is the smallest product version that can be released to validate
whether the idea solves a real customer problem.**

A common misunderstanding:

MVP does **not** mean:

❌ Poor quality product\
❌ Incomplete product\
❌ A product with only minimum features

MVP means:

✅ Minimum effort\
✅ Maximum learning\
✅ Real customer feedback

**2. Why Do We Need MVP?**

Building a complete product is expensive.

Before investing heavily, companies need to validate:

-   Is this problem real?

-   Do users need this solution?

-   Will customers use it?

-   Will customers pay for it?

Without MVP:

Company spends:

₹5 crore

18 months development

Then discovers:

Nobody needs the product.

With MVP:

Company tests the idea early.

**3. MVP Mindset**

Traditional thinking:

\"Let\'s build everything and launch.\"

MVP thinking:

\"Let\'s build the smallest valuable solution, learn, and improve.\"

Product cycle:

Build

↓

Measure

↓

Learn

↓

Improve

↓

Repeat

**4. MVP vs Prototype vs Proof of Concept**

Very important interview topic.

**Prototype**

Purpose:

Test design or user experience.

Question:

\"Can users understand this?\"

Example:

Clickable Figma design of AI dashboard.

**Proof of Concept (POC)**

Purpose:

Test technical feasibility.

Question:

\"Can we build this technology?\"

Example:

Testing whether AI can extract drug names from documents.

**MVP**

Purpose:

Test real customer value.

Question:

\"Will users use this solution?\"

Example:

Working AI extraction tool used by safety associates.

Comparison:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
            **Prototype**         **POC**            **MVP**
  --------- --------------------- ------------------ ---------------------
  Goal      Test idea/design      Test technology    Test market value

  Users     Internal/test users   Technical team     Real customers

  Output    Mockup                Experiment         Working product
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Lesson 9: Product Prioritization Frameworks**

**1. What is Product Prioritization?**

**Product Prioritization** is the process of deciding:

-   Which problems to solve first

-   Which features to build first

-   Which initiatives should receive resources

A Product Manager always faces:

-   Limited time

-   Limited developers

-   Limited budget

-   Many customer requests

The PM\'s job is not:

\"Build everything.\"

The PM\'s job is:

**\"Build the things that create the highest value.\"**

**2. Why Prioritization Matters**

Without prioritization:

❌ Teams build low-value features\
❌ Important problems are delayed\
❌ Resources are wasted\
❌ Product becomes complicated

Good prioritization helps:

✅ Maximize customer value\
✅ Achieve business goals\
✅ Reduce development waste\
✅ Make transparent decisions

Safety associates need faster workflow.

**1. User Impact**

**2. Business Value**

Does it support company goals?

How much engineering work is needed?

**4. Risk**

Does it reduce risk or create risk?

**5. Strategic Alignment**

Does it support product vision?

**5. MoSCoW Prioritization**

One of the most common Agile frameworks.

MoSCoW stands for:

**M - Must Have**

Critical features.

-   Document upload

**S - Should Have**

Important but not immediately critical.

Example:

-   Duplicate detection

**C - Could Have**

Nice improvements.

Example:

-   Custom dashboard themes

**W - Won\'t Have (Now)**

Not included currently.

Example:

Example:

  **Feature**                                    **Priority**

  Theme customization                            Could
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**6. RICE Scoring Framework ⭐**

Very popular in product companies.

RICE means:

**R - Reach**

How many users will benefit?

Example:

1000 users/month

**I - Impact**

How much impact?

Scale:

Low → High

**C - Confidence**

How confident are we?

Example:

Based on customer research.

**E - Effort**

How much work required?

Formula:

RICE Score =

(Reach × Impact × Confidence)

÷

Effort

**Example:**

Feature:

AI case extraction

Reach:

1000 users

Impact:

3

Confidence:

80%

Effort:

5

Score:

(1000 × 3 × 0.8) ÷ 5

=

480

Higher score = Higher priority.

**7. WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First)**

Common in Agile scaling frameworks like SAFe.

Purpose:

Deliver maximum economic value quickly.

Formula:

WSJF = Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size

Cost of Delay includes:

**User/Business Value**

How valuable?

**Time Criticality**

How urgent?

**Risk Reduction**

Does it reduce risk?

Example:

Regulatory reporting automation:

High:

-   Business value

-   Compliance importance

-   Risk reduction

Therefore:

High priority.

**Lesson 12: A/B Testing and Experimentation**

**1. What is A/B Testing?**

**A/B Testing** is a method of comparing two versions of a product,
feature, or experience to determine which one performs better.

Simple definition:

**A/B testing allows Product Managers to make decisions based on user
behavior instead of assumptions.**

Example:

You have two versions of an AI case review screen:

**Version A:**

-   Current design

**Version B:**

-   New AI-assisted design

Users are randomly divided into two groups.

The PM measures which version creates better results.

**2. Why Do Product Teams Run Experiments?**

Product decisions often involve uncertainty.

Example:

Question:

\"Will adding AI suggestions improve reviewer efficiency?\"

Instead of guessing:

Run an experiment.

A/B testing helps:

✅ Validate ideas\
✅ Reduce product risk\
✅ Improve user experience\
✅ Increase adoption\
✅ Make data-driven decisions

**Step 5: Analyze Results**

**Step 6: Decide**

Possible outcomes:

2.  Improve and test again

3.  Keep A
