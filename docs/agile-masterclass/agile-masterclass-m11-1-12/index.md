# Agile Masterclass_M11 (1-12)

**MODULE 11 -- Scaling Agile**

**Lesson 1: Introduction to Agile Scaling**

**1. Why Do We Need Agile Scaling?**

In previous modules, we learned how Agile works at a **team level**.

Example:

A Scrum team has:

-   Product Owner

-   Scrum Master

-   Developers

-   Testers

-   Designers

The team works in:

-   2-week sprints

-   Daily stand-ups

-   Sprint planning

-   Sprint review

-   Retrospective

This works very well when **one team builds one product**.

But what happens when:

A large organization has 500+ people building one complex product?

Example:

A healthcare AI company wants to build an:

**AI-powered Drug Safety Monitoring Platform**

It needs:

-   AI/ML team

-   Data engineering team

-   Pharmacovigilance experts

-   Mobile app team

-   Backend team

-   Security team

-   Compliance team

-   Regulatory team

-   DevOps team

One Scrum team cannot handle this.

This is where **Agile Scaling** comes in.

**2. What is Agile Scaling?**

**Simple Definition:**

**Agile Scaling is the practice of applying Agile principles across
multiple teams, departments, and the entire organization while
maintaining speed, collaboration, and business alignment.**

In simple words:

\"How do we make Agile work when hundreds or thousands of people are
involved?\"

**Small Agile vs Scaled Agile**

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Small Agile Team**          **Scaled Agile Organization**
  ----------------------------- -----------------------------------------
  1 Scrum Team                  Multiple Scrum Teams

  5-10 members                  Hundreds of members

  One Product Owner             Multiple Product Owners

  One Backlog                   Multiple connected backlogs

  One Sprint                    Multiple synchronized teams

  Team decisions                Organization-level decisions
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Lesson 2: SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework)**

**1. Introduction to SAFe**

**What is SAFe?**

**SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework)** is the most widely adopted framework
for applying Agile practices across large organizations.

Simple definition:

**SAFe is a framework that helps hundreds or thousands of people work
together using Agile principles while aligning business strategy,
product development, and delivery.**

**Simple Example**

A startup has:

1 Product

1 Scrum Team

10 People

Normal Scrum is enough.

But imagine:

A global pharmaceutical company building an:

**AI Drug Discovery & Pharmacovigilance Platform**

It has:

500 Employees

50 Developers

20 Data Scientists

30 QA Engineers

15 Pharmacologists

10 Regulatory Experts

20 Product People

50 Business Stakeholders

Scrum alone cannot coordinate everyone.

SAFe provides the structure.

**2. Why Was SAFe Created?**

Traditional Agile worked well for small teams.

But enterprises faced problems:

**Problem 1: Business and Development Disconnect**

Business says:

\"We need an AI safety monitoring system.\"

Development says:

\"We are building technical features.\"

Both are working separately.

SAFe connects:

Business Strategy

\|

\|

Product Development

\|

\|

Delivery Teams

**Problem 2: Multiple Agile Teams**

Example:

A healthcare AI product has:

Team A → AI Model

Team B → Data Pipeline

Team C → Web Application

Team D → Mobile App

Team E → Compliance

Without coordination:

-   Teams block each other

-   Features don\'t integrate

-   Deadlines slip

SAFe creates alignment.

**Problem 3: Enterprise Priorities**

A company may have 100 projects.

Question:

Which one gets priority?

SAFe introduces:

-   Portfolio Management

-   Lean budgeting

-   Strategic alignment

**4. SAFe Core Principles**

SAFe is based on Lean-Agile principles.

Important principles:

**Principle 1: Take an Economic View**

Meaning:

Always consider business value.

Example:

Feature A:

Takes 6 months.

Value: Low.

Feature B:

Takes 2 months.

Value: High.

Choose B.

**Principle 2: Apply Systems Thinking**

Don\'t optimize one team.

Optimize the entire system.

Example:

Developer team is fast.

But regulatory approval takes 6 months.

Total delivery is still slow.

**Principle 3: Build Incrementally**

Instead of:

\"Build everything for 2 years.\"

Deliver:

Month 1:

Basic AI model

Month 3:

Clinical dashboard

Month 6:

Regulatory automation

**Principle 4: Decentralize Decision Making**

Teams should make technical decisions.

Leadership provides direction.

**Principle 5: Organize Around Value**

Traditional:

Department → Projects

SAFe:

Value Stream → Customer Outcome

**Lesson 4: PI Planning (Program Increment Planning)**

**1. Introduction to PI Planning**

In the previous lesson, we learned about **Agile Release Train (ART)**.

ART brings multiple teams together.

But a question remains:

\"How do 10 different Agile teams decide what they will build together
for the next few months?\"

The answer is:

**PI Planning**

**Simple Definition:**

**PI Planning (Program Increment Planning) is a SAFe event where all
teams in an Agile Release Train come together to plan the next Program
Increment, align on goals, identify dependencies, and create a delivery
plan.**

In simple words:

**PI Planning is a big planning meeting where 50--125 people decide how
they will work together for the next 8--12 weeks.**

**2. What is a Program Increment (PI)?**

Before understanding PI Planning, understand PI.

**Program Increment:**

A **Program Increment** is a fixed time period during which an ART
delivers value.

Usually:

8--12 Weeks

Example:

A healthcare AI company creates:

**PI Objective:**

\"Launch AI-powered adverse event detection version 2.\"

During this PI:

Teams deliver:

-   New AI model

-   Faster data processing

-   Improved dashboard

-   Regulatory reports

**3. Why Do We Need PI Planning?**

Without PI Planning:

Imagine:

AI Team:

\"We are building a new ML model.\"

Data Team:

\"We changed the database.\"

Application Team:

\"We expected a different API.\"

Compliance Team:

\"We need more documentation.\"

Result:

❌ Delays\
❌ Rework\
❌ Conflicts

PI Planning creates:

✅ Shared understanding\
✅ Team alignment\
✅ Dependency visibility\
✅ Common goals

**4. PI Planning Participants**

**Lesson 6: LeSS (Large Scale Scrum)**

**1. Introduction to LeSS**

In previous lessons, we learned:

-   **SAFe** → Enterprise-level Agile scaling framework

-   **ART** → Multiple teams working together

-   **PI Planning** → Alignment event in SAFe

Now we will learn another scaling framework:

**LeSS (Large Scale Scrum)**

**Simple Definition:**

**LeSS is a framework that extends Scrum principles to multiple teams
while keeping Scrum simple, lightweight, and focused on one product.**

In simple words:

**LeSS says: \"Do not add unnecessary complexity. Just scale Scrum with
multiple teams working on one product.\"**

**2. Why Was LeSS Created?**

Many organizations tried to scale Agile by adding:

-   More roles

-   More meetings

-   More processes

-   More management layers

The result:

Agile became similar to traditional management.

Example:

Original Scrum:

\|

Scrum Team

\|

After excessive scaling:

Manager

\|

Program Manager

\|

Coordinator

\|

Team Lead

\|

Scrum Team

Too much complexity.

LeSS was created to solve this problem.

**3. LeSS Philosophy**

LeSS follows one major idea:

**\"More teams, but still one product and one Scrum.\"**

**LeSS Principles**

Important principles:

**Principle 1: Large-Scale Scrum is Still Scrum**

LeSS does not create a new Agile method.

It keeps Scrum values:

-   Transparency

-   Inspection

-   Adaptation

**Principle 2: One Product, One Product Owner**

In LeSS:

There is:

✅ One Product Owner\
✅ One Product Backlog\
✅ One Product Vision

Example:

Healthcare AI Platform:

Wrong approach:

AI Team → Own Product Backlog

Data Team → Own Product Backlog

App Team → Own Product Backlog

LeSS approach:

One Product Owner

\|

One Product Backlog

\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\--

\| \| \|

**Principle 3: Customer Focus**

Teams focus on:

Customer value, not department goals.

Example:

Customer need:

\"Detect adverse drug reactions faster.\"

Not:

\"AI team must finish 10 models.\"

**Principle 4: Remove Unnecessary Roles**

LeSS avoids adding many management layers.

Example:

SAFe:

-   RTE

-   Product Management

-   Business Owners

LeSS:

Keeps:

-   Product Owner

**Simple Definition:**

**Nexus is a framework that helps 3--9 Scrum teams work together on a
single product while managing dependencies and integration challenges.**

In simple words:

**Nexus is Scrum for multiple teams building one product together.**

**2. Who Created Nexus?**

Nexus was created by:

Scrum.org

It was introduced by:

Ken Schwaber

(the co-creator of Scrum)

The goal:

Extend Scrum without creating a complex enterprise framework.

**3. Why Was Nexus Created?**

Normal Scrum works well:

1 Product

1 Scrum Team

5-10 People

But imagine:

A healthcare AI company building:

**AI Clinical Decision Support Platform**

Now we have:

Team 1 → AI Models

Team 2 → Data Platform

Team 3 → Mobile Application

Team 4 → Backend Services

Team 5 → Testing

Problems appear:

**Problem 1: Integration Issues**

Example:

AI team creates a model.

Application team cannot connect because API is different.

**Problem 2: Dependency Management**

Example:

AI team needs:

Patient data pipeline.

Data team is delayed.

**Problem 3: Multiple Team Priorities**

Each team focuses on its own work.

But customer needs:

A complete product.

Nexus solves these problems.

**4. Nexus Philosophy**

Nexus follows one major idea:

**Multiple Scrum teams should behave like one integrated team.**

Core principles:

**1. One Product**

There is:

✅ One Product Goal\
✅ One Product Backlog\
✅ One Product Owner

**2. Integrated Increment**

At the end of every Sprint:

The product should work as one complete system.

Example:

Wrong:

AI Model completed

Database completed

UI completed

But nothing works together.

Correct:

AI Model

\+

Database

\+

UI

=

Working Product Increment

-   One Product Owner

**What is NIT?**
