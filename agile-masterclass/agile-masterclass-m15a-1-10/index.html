    Agile Masterclass_M15A (1-10)¶

    Lesson 1: Business Case & Feasibility Analysis (Masterclass)

    1. Why This Topic Matters for a Business Analyst

    Many beginners think:

    \"BA receives requirements and writes documents.\"

    That is a junior BA mindset.

    Industry BA works earlier:

    Business Problem → Opportunity → Business Case → Feasibility → Requirements → Solution

    Example:

    A pharmaceutical company says:

    \"We want an AI system to detect adverse drug reactions automatically.\"

    A junior BA asks:

    \"What fields should the form contain?\"

    A senior BA asks:

    \"Why do we need this system? What business problem exists? What value will AI create? Is it feasible?\"

    2. What is a Business Case?

    A Business Case is a document that explains:

    \"Why should the organization invest money, time, and resources into this initiative?\"

    It answers:

    • Why are we doing this?

    • What problem are we solving?

    • What benefits will we get?

    • How much will it cost?

    • What risks exist?

    • Should we proceed or not?

    Simple Formula

    Business Case

    =

    Problem

    +

    Opportunity

    +

    Solution Options

    +

    Cost

    +

    Benefits

    +

    Risk

    +

    Recommendation

    3. Business Case vs Requirement Document

    Many BA beginners confuse these.

    Business Case Requirement Document

    Explains WHY Explains WHAT

    Created before project approval Created after approval

    Business focused Solution focused

    Used by executives Used by project teams

    Contains ROI Contains features/functions

    Example:

    Business Case

    Problem:

    Pharmacovigilance team manually reviews 50,000 safety reports monthly.

    Impact:

    • 30 days processing time

    • High operational cost

    • Risk of delayed reporting

    Proposal:

    AI-powered adverse event detection platform

    Expected benefit:

    • Reduce review time by 70%

    • Improve compliance

    Requirement Document

    Functional requirement:

    System shall classify adverse event reports into seriousness categories.

    4. Components of a Business Case

    A professional BA business case usually contains:

    Section 1: Executive Summary

    One-page overview.

    Example:

    Project:

    AI Pharmacovigilance Intelligence Platform

    Objective:

    Reduce manual adverse event review using AI.

    Expected Outcome:

    Decrease processing time from 30 days to 10 days.

    Section 2: Business Problem Statement

    A good BA defines the problem clearly.

    Bad:

    \"We need AI.\"

    Good:

    \"The pharmacovigilance team currently spends 60% of its time manually reviewing incoming safety reports, causing delays in signal detection.\"

    Problem Statement Template

    Currently:

    Who is affected?

    What problem exists?

    How often does it occur?

    What is the business impact?

    Why solve now?

    Section 3: Current State Analysis (AS-IS)

    BA documents current process.

    Example:

    Current ADR processing:

    Doctor Report

    Safety Associate Review

    ↓

    Manual Coding

    |

    ↓

    Medical Review

    |

    ↓

    Regulatory Submission

    Problems:

    • Manual entry errors

    • Slow processing

    • Duplicate work

    Section 4: Future State (TO-BE)

    After improvement:

    ADR Report

    |

    ↓

    AI NLP Extraction

    |

    ↓

    Auto Classification

    |

    ↓

    Human Validation

    |

    ↓

    Regulatory Submission

    Section 5: Solution Options Analysis

    BA does not directly jump to one solution.

    Example:

    Problem:

    Slow adverse event processing.

    Possible options:

    Option Description

    Option 1 Hire more analysts

    Option 2 Improve existing software

    Option 3 Build AI system

    Then compare.

    Section 6: Feasibility Analysis

    This is a major BA responsibility.

    There are five feasibility types.

    Remember:

    TELOS Framework

    T - Technical

    E - Economic

    L - Legal

    O - Operational

    S - Schedule

    1. Technical Feasibility

    Question:

    Can we build this technology?

    Example:

    AI Pharmacovigilance System:

    Check:

    • Data availability

    • AI model capability

    • Cloud infrastructure

    • Integration capability

    2. Economic Feasibility

    Question:

    Does benefit justify cost?

    Example:

    Cost:

    AI Development

    \$300,000

    Annual Maintenance

    \$50,000

    Benefits:

    Reduced manual effort

    \$250,000/year

    Reduced compliance risk

    \$150,000/year

    ROI calculation:

    ROI =

    (Benefit - Cost)

    -------------

    Cost

    3. Legal Feasibility

    Especially important in healthcare.

    Questions:

    • GDPR compliance?

    • HIPAA?

    • Patient privacy?

    • Data retention rules?

    • Regulatory requirements?

    4. Operational Feasibility

    Question:

    Will users actually adopt it?

    Example:

    Safety associates may resist AI.

    BA checks:

    • Training needs

    • Process changes

    • User acceptance

    5. Schedule Feasibility

    Question:

    Can we deliver within required timeline?

    Example:

    Regulatory deadline:

    6 months

    Development estimate:

    12 months

    Project not feasible.

    7. Cost-Benefit Analysis

    BA compares:

    Costs

    Direct Cost

    • Development

    • Software licenses

    • Cloud cost

    • Training

    Indirect Cost

    • Process change

    • Migration

    • Maintenance

    Benefits

    Tangible Benefits

    Can be measured.

    Example:

    • Save employee hours

    • Reduce operational cost

    Intangible Benefits

    Harder to measure.

    Example:

    • Better customer trust

    • Improved compliance reputation

    8. SWOT Analysis in Business Case

    BA often uses SWOT.

    Strengths

    Internal advantages

    Existing healthcare data

    Weaknesses

    Internal limitations

    Example:

    Poor data quality

    Opportunities

    External benefits

    Example:

    AI adoption

    Threats

    External risks

    Example:

    Regulatory changes

    Lesson 2: Requirement Prioritization Masterclass

    1. Why Requirement Prioritization Matters for a BA

    In real projects, stakeholders rarely give 5--10 requirements.

    They give hundreds.

    Example:

    A pharmaceutical company wants an AI Pharmacovigilance Platform.

    Stakeholders request:

    • AI adverse event extraction

    • Mobile app

    • Dashboard

    • Voice reporting

    • Multiple language support

    • Blockchain audit trail

    • Chatbot

    • Automated regulatory submission

    • Patient portal

    • Predictive safety signals

    But:

    • Budget is limited

    • Timeline is limited

    • Team capacity is limited

    The BA must answer:

    \"Which requirements should we build first?\"

    This is Requirement Prioritization.

    2. What is Requirement Prioritization?

    Requirement prioritization is the process of:

    Evaluating requirements based on business value, urgency, risk, cost, and feasibility to decide implementation order.

    Simple Formula

    Priority

    =

    Business Value

    +

    Urgency

    +

    Risk Reduction

    -

    Cost

    -

    Complexity

    3. Who Prioritizes Requirements?

    A BA does not prioritize alone.

    Usually:

    Business Sponsor

    |

    |

    Product Owner

    |

    |

    Business Analyst

    |

    |

    Development Team

    |

    |

    Users

    Different stakeholders bring different perspectives.

    4. Factors Used for Prioritization

    A BA evaluates:

    1. Business Value

    Question:

    How much value does this provide?

    Example:

    Automatic adverse event classification:

    High value.

    2. User Impact

    Question:

    How many users benefit?

    Example:

    Dashboard used by 500 safety analysts.

    High impact.

    3. Regulatory Importance

    Important in healthcare.

    Example:

    FDA reporting compliance.

    High priority.

    4. Risk Reduction

    Question:

    Does this reduce business risk?

    Example:

    AI duplicate case detection.

    Reduces compliance risk.

    5. Cost

    Question:

    How expensive is implementation?

    6. Complexity

    Question:

    How difficult is it?

    5. Common Requirement Prioritization Techniques

    A professional BA should know multiple techniques.

    Technique 1: MoSCoW Prioritization

    Most commonly used in Agile.

    MoSCoW means:

    M - Must Have

    S - Should Have

    C - Could Have

    W - Won\'t Have Now

    A) Must Have

    Critical requirements.

    Without this, product fails.

    Example:

    AI Pharmacovigilance MVP:

    ✅ Case ingestion\ ✅ Adverse event extraction\ ✅ User authentication\ ✅ Audit logging

    B) Should Have

    Important but not critical.

    Example:

    • Advanced analytics dashboard

    • Export reports

    C) Could Have

    Nice to have.

    Example:

    • Mobile app

    • Voice commands

    D) Won\'t Have Now

    Deferred.

    Example:

    • Blockchain integration

    MoSCoW Example

    Requirement Priority

    Login Must

    ADR extraction Must

    Dashboard Should

    Mobile App Could

    Blockchain Won\'t

    Technique 2: Value vs Effort Matrix

    Very popular with Product Managers.

    Two dimensions:

    Value

    How much business benefit?

    Effort

    How much work required?

    Matrix:

    HIGH VALUE

    |

    Quick Wins | Strategic Projects

    |

    -----------------|-----------------

    |

    Low Priority | Avoid / Later

    |

    LOW VALUE

    LOW EFFORT -------- HIGH EFFORT

    Example:

    Feature Value Effort Decision

    ADR extraction High Medium Build

    Voice bot Medium High Later

    Login High Low Immediate

    Blockchain Low High Avoid

    Technique 3: Weighted Scoring Model

    Used in enterprise organizations.

    BA assigns scores.

    Example criteria:

    Criteria Weight

    Business Value 40%

    User Impact 25%

    Risk Reduction 20%

    Complexity 15%

    Example:

    Requirement:

    AI Signal Detection

    Scores:

    Factor Score

    Business Value 9

    User Impact 8

    Risk Reduction 10

    Complexity 6

    Calculation:

    Priority Score =

    (Value × Weight)

    +

    (Impact × Weight)

    +

    (Risk × Weight)

    -

    (Complexity × Weight)

    Highest score gets priority.

    Technique 4: RICE Framework

    Used heavily in Product Management.

    RICE:

    R = Reach

    I = Impact

    C = Confidence

    E = Effort

    Formula:

    RICE Score

    =

    Reach × Impact × Confidence

    --------------------------

    Effort

    Example:

    Feature:

    AI duplicate case detection

    Reach:

    1000 users

    Impact:

    3

    Confidence:

    80%

    Effort:

    5 months

    Score:

    1000 × 3 × 0.8

    --------------

    5

    =480

    Technique 5: Kano Model

    Focuses on customer satisfaction.

    Three major categories:

    1. Basic Needs

    Expected features.

    Missing → unhappy.

    Example:

    Secure login.

    2. Performance Needs

    More is better.

    Example:

    Faster report generation.

    3. Delighters

    Unexpected value.

    Example:

    AI recommendation engine.

    Diagram:

    Customer Satisfaction

    \^

    |

    Delighter

    |

    Performance

    |

    Basic ---------------->

    Functionality

    Technique 6: WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First)

    Used in SAFe Agile.

    Formula:

    WSJF =

    Cost of Delay

    -------------

    Job Size

    Where:

    Cost of Delay:

    Business Value

    +

    Time Criticality

    +

    Risk Reduction

    Example:

    Feature A:

    Business Value = 8

    Time Criticality = 7

    Risk Reduction = 9

    Job Size = 4

    Cost of Delay:

    8+7+9=24

    WSJF:

    24/4 = 6

    Higher WSJF = higher priority.

    Lesson 3: Requirement Traceability Matrix (RTM) Masterclass

    1. Why RTM is Important for a Business Analyst

    In real projects, requirements change continuously.

    Example:

    A pharmaceutical company builds an AI Pharmacovigilance System.

    Initial requirement:

    \"System should automatically classify adverse events.\"

    Later:

    Regulatory team says:

    \"System must also maintain an audit trail showing why AI made each classification.\"

    Now questions arise:

    • Was this requirement captured?

    • Was it developed?

    • Was it tested?

    • Did it meet compliance needs?

    • Who approved the change?

    This is where RTM helps.

    2. What is Requirement Traceability Matrix (RTM)?

    A Requirement Traceability Matrix is a document that connects:

    Business Requirements → Functional Requirements → User Stories → Development Tasks → Test Cases → Deployment

    It ensures every requirement is:

    ✅ Identified\ ✅ Implemented\ ✅ Tested\ ✅ Approved

    Simple Definition

    RTM is a tracking document that proves that every business requirement has been delivered and validated.

    4. Why Organizations Use RTM

    1. Prevent Missing Requirements

    Example:

    Business requested:

    \"Generate regulatory reports.\"

    Development built:

    \"Generate internal dashboard.\"

    RTM catches this mismatch.

    2. Support Testing

    QA can verify:

    \"Which test validates this requirement?\"

    3. Manage Changes

    When a requirement changes:

    BA can identify:

    • Which features are impacted?

    • Which test cases need updates?

    • Which documents need changes?

    4. Compliance

    Highly important in:

    • Healthcare

    • Banking

    • Aviation

    • Pharma

    Regulators ask:

    \"Show evidence that requirements were tested.\"

    RTM provides proof.

    Lesson 4: Business Rules & Decision Modeling Masterclass

    1. Why Business Rules Matter for a BA

    A modern system does not only store data or perform actions.

    It makes decisions.

    Examples:

    A banking system decides:

    • Approve or reject loan?

    • Calculate interest rate?

    • Detect fraud?

    A healthcare system decides:

    • Is an adverse event serious?

    • Does a case require expedited reporting?

    • Should a safety signal be escalated?

    These decisions are controlled by Business Rules.

    2. What is a Business Rule?

    A business rule is a statement that defines:

    \"How an organization makes decisions, operates, or enforces policies.\"

    Simple Examples

    Banking

    IF customer credit score > 750

    THEN loan eligibility = High

    Insurance

    IF claim amount > ₹5 lakh

    THEN manager approval required

    Pharmacovigilance

    IF adverse event = Serious

    AND patient outcome = Death

    THEN expedited regulatory reporting required

    3. Business Rule vs Requirement

    Many BAs confuse these.

    Business Rule Requirement

    Defines decision logic Defines system capability

    Business policy System behavior

    3. What is a KPI?

    A Key Performance Indicator (KPI) is a metric that measures progress toward an important business objective.

    Example:

    Metric:

    Average review time = 18 minutes

    KPI:

    Reduce average review time to under 15 minutes by the end of Q4.

    Every KPI is a metric, but not every metric is a KPI.

    Term Purpose

    Metric Measures something

    KPI Measures success against a business objective

    OKR (Objectives & Key Goal-setting framework Results)

    Objective:

    Improve pharmacovigilance efficiency.

    Key Results:

    • Reduce manual review time by 40%

    • Increase AI accuracy to 95%

    • Reduce duplicate reports by 50%

    KPIs are then tracked to determine whether these key results are being achieved.

    • Specific

    • Measurable

    • Achievable

    • Relevant

    • Time-bound

    Increase AI classification accuracy from 90% to 95% within six months.

    1. Why Workshop Facilitation is Important

    2. What is a BA Workshop?

    A BA Workshop is:

    A structured meeting where stakeholders collaborate to discover, analyze, prioritize, validate, or approve business requirements and solutions.

    10. Stakeholder Management During Workshops

    Stakeholder Type BA Approach

    Technical Lead Clarify feasibility and constraints

    11. Managing Conflicts

    Conflict is normal.

    Example:

    Regulatory Team:

    \"We need every AI recommendation manually reviewed.\"

    Product Team:

    \"Manual review slows everything down.\"

    The BA should:

    • Clarify the underlying business need.

    • Identify regulatory constraints.

    • Explore alternatives.

    • Evaluate risks.

    • Facilitate agreement.

    Focus on solving the problem, not \"winning\" the argument.

    12. Common Facilitation Techniques

    Brainstorming

    Generate ideas without immediate evaluation.

    Affinity Mapping

    Group similar ideas into themes.

    Example:

    Ideas:

    • Faster processing

    • Better dashboards

    • AI suggestions

    • Mobile access

    Themes:

    • Automation

    • Reporting

    • User Experience

    MoSCoW Prioritization

    Rank requirements:

    • Must

    • Should

    • Could

    • Won\'t

    Dot Voting

    Each participant receives votes to prioritize ideas.

    Useful when many options exist.

    Five Whys

    Identify root causes.

    Problem:

    Late regulatory submissions.

    Why?

    Manual review delays.

    Why?

    Too many duplicate reports.

    Continue asking \"Why?\" until the underlying cause is identified.
