    MODULE 12 -- Lesson 1

    How AI Changes Agile

    1. Introduction

    Traditional Agile was created mainly for:

    • Software applications

    • Websites

    • Mobile apps

    • Business systems

    Example:

    Building an e-commerce application.

    Requirements:

    • Login

    • Search

    • Cart

    • Payment

    The team can clearly define:

    \"What should be built.\"

    AI products are different.

    Example:

    Building:

    AI-powered drug safety assistant

    The question changes:

    From:

    \"What features should we build?\"

    To:

    \"How can we create a system that learns, predicts, and improves?\"

    2. Why AI Changes Agile?

    AI introduces uncertainty.

    Traditional software:

    Input

    ↓

    Rules

    ↓

    Output

    Example:

    If user clicks button:

    → Show dashboard

    AI system:

    Data

    ↓

    AI Model

    ↓

    Prediction

    ↓

    Feedback

    ↓

    Model Improvement

    The output is probabilistic.

    Example:

    AI Drug Safety System:

    Input:

    Adverse event report

    AI prediction:

    \"Possible liver toxicity\"

    Confidence:

    87%

    Human expert:

    Approve / Reject

    3. Traditional Agile vs AI Agile

    Important interview topic.

    Traditional Agile AI + Agile

    Requirement driven Experiment driven

    Fixed functionality Learning system

    Code is main asset Data + Model + Code

    Testing software behavior Testing model performance

    Predictable output Probabilistic output

    Feature delivery Model improvement

    4. Software Feature vs AI Capability

    Traditional Agile:

    User Story:

    I want password reset,

    • How accurate should it be?

    • What data is required?

    • How will errors be handled?

    • How will humans validate results?

    13. Interview Questions

    Answer:

    AI changes Agile from feature-focused development to experiment-driven development where teams continuously improve models using data and feedback.

    Q2. Difference between traditional software Agile and AI Agile?

    Answer:

    Traditional Agile focuses on building predictable functionality, while AI Agile focuses on developing learning systems where data, models, and continuous validation are important.

    Answer:

    How to Build an AI Product Using Agile

    Simple Definition:

    AI Product Development is the process of creating products that use Artificial Intelligence to solve customer problems by combining data, algorithms, software, and human feedback.

    In simple words:

    An AI product is not just an application. It is a combination of software + data + models + continuous learning.

    4. AI Product Lifecycle

    AI products follow a continuous lifecycle.

    Phase 1: Problem Discovery

    Question:

    \"Should we use AI?\"

    Example:

    Pharmacovigilance problem:

    Current process:

    Safety experts manually review thousands of reports.

    Problem:

    Slow signal detection.

    AI opportunity:

    Use NLP to identify safety signals.

    Phase 2: Define AI Product Vision

    A Product Manager defines:

    Vision:

    \"Reduce adverse drug reaction detection time using AI.\"

    Not:

    \"Build an AI model.\"

    Because AI is a solution, not the goal.

    Phase 3: Data Discovery

    AI depends on data.

    Questions:

    • What data exists?

    • Is it reliable?

    • Is it enough?

    • Is it legally usable?

    Healthcare example:

    Data sources:

    • Electronic health records

    • Clinical trial data

    • Medical publications

    • Safety reports

    Phase 4: AI Feasibility Assessment

    Before building:

    Ask:

    1. Is AI suitable?

    Example:

    Problem:

    Calculate BMI.

    AI is unnecessary.

    Simple formula works.

    Problem:

    Predict patient risk.

    AI may help.

    2. Is enough data available?

    No data:

    No AI.

    3. Is accuracy acceptable?

    Healthcare requires high confidence.

    Phase 5: Build AI MVP

    Important concept.

    AI MVP = Minimum Viable AI Product

    Not:

    \"Build perfect AI.\"

    Instead:

    \"Build enough AI to test value.\"

    Example:

    Big vision:

    AI Pharmacovigilance Platform

    MVP:

    AI extracts adverse drug events from reports.

    Later:

    • Risk prediction

    • Automated reporting

    • Regulatory intelligence

    Phase 6: Model Development

    Data Science team works on:

    Data Preparation

    Clean and organize data.

    Example:

    Remove duplicate safety reports.

    Feature Engineering

    Select useful information.

    Example:

    Drug name + symptom + patient age.

    Model Training

    AI learns patterns.

    Model Evaluation

    Measure:

    • Accuracy

    • Precision

    • Recall

    Phase 7: AI Product Validation

    Important difference:

    Software testing asks:

    \"Does it work?\"

    AI validation asks:

    \"How well does it perform?\"

    Example:

    AI detects adverse events.

    Evaluation:

    1000 historical reports tested.

    Results:

    • Correct detection: 920

    Phase 8: Deployment

    AI moves into production.

    Example:

    Safety officers use AI assistant.

    Need:

    • Monitoring

    • Security

    • Compliance

    AI products improve continuously.

    Lesson 3: Large Language Models (LLMs)

    1. Introduction to Large Language Models

    In previous lessons, we learned:

    • How AI changes Agile

    • AI Product Development lifecycle

    • AI Product Manager and BA roles

    Now we learn the foundation behind modern AI products:

    Large Language Models (LLMs)

    Simple Definition:

    A Large Language Model (LLM) is an AI model trained on massive amounts of text data to understand, generate, summarize, translate, and reason with human language.

    In simple words:

    LLM is a very advanced language engine that learns patterns from huge amounts of text and uses those patterns to generate useful responses.

    2. Why Are LLMs Important?

    Before LLMs:

    Software followed fixed rules.

    Example:

    Customer support chatbot:

    User: I forgot my password

    System:

    If password issue →

    Show reset link

    Limited.

    After LLMs:

    User:

    I cannot access my account because I changed my phone.

    AI:

    Understands the situation,

    asks questions,

    guides user through recovery.

    LLMs allow:

    • Natural conversations

    • Content generation

    • Document analysis

    • Knowledge extraction

    • AI assistants

    3. Evolution of AI Language Systems

    Understanding history helps in interviews.

    Stage 1: Rule-Based Systems

    Example:

    IF user says \"hello\"

    4. How Does an LLM Work? (Simple Explanation)

    An LLM learns language patterns.

    Example:

    Sentence:

    \"The patient received a dose of...\"

    The model predicts:

    Possible next words:

    • medication

    • treatment

    • drug

    It learns:

    • Grammar

    • Context

    • Relationships between words

    • Information patterns

    Basic process:

    Large Text Data

    ↓

    Training

    ↓

    Language Patterns Learned

    ↓

    LLM Model

    ↓

    Generate Response

    5. What Does \"Large\" Mean in LLM?

    Large refers to:

    1. Training Data

    Millions/billions of documents.

    Examples:

    • Books

    • Websites

    • Research papers

    • Code

    • Medical literature

    2. Parameters

    Parameters are internal values the model learns.

    Example:

    Small model:

    Millions of parameters.

    Large model:

    Billions of parameters.

    More parameters generally allow:

    • Better understanding

    • Better generation ability

    6. Transformer Architecture

    Important interview concept.

    Modern LLMs are based on:

    Transformer Architecture

    Introduced in the paper:

    \"Attention Is All You Need\"

    The key innovation:

    Attention Mechanism

    Simple explanation:

    Attention helps the model understand which words are important.

    Example:

    Sentence:

    \"The patient took the drug because it reduced pain.\"

    The model understands:

    \"it\" refers to the drug.

    Without attention:

    AI may lose context.

    7. Tokens

    LLMs do not read text exactly like humans.

    They process:

    Tokens

    A token is a small piece of text.

    Example:

    Sentence:

    AI helps doctors

    May become:

    AI

    helps

    doctors

    (tokens)

    LLMs have token limits.

    Example:

    A document with thousands of pages may exceed the model\'s context window.

    8. Training an LLM

    LLM training happens in stages.

    Stage 1: Pre-training

    The model learns from massive text data.

    Goal:

    Learn language patterns.

    Example:

    Predict next word.

    Input:

    \"The capital of India is\"

    Model learns:

    \"New Delhi\"

    Stage 2: Fine-Tuning

    The model is trained for specific tasks.

    Example:

    General LLM:

    Understands language.

    Healthcare fine-tuned LLM:

    Understands:

    • Medical terminology

    • Clinical documentation

    • Drug information

    Stage 3: Alignment

    The model is adjusted to:

    • Follow instructions

    • Be safer

    • Provide useful answers

    Simple Definition:

    Human-in-the-Loop AI is an AI system where humans actively participate in reviewing, correcting, guiding, or approving AI decisions.

    In simple words:

    AI provides intelligence, but humans provide judgment.

    2. Why Do We Need Humans in AI?

    AI is powerful but not perfect.

    AI can:

    • Process huge amounts of data

    • Generate predictions

    But AI may not understand:

    • Business context

    • Ethical concerns

    • Medical responsibility

    • Rare situations

    Example:

    AI Pharmacovigilance System:

    \"Possible liver toxicity signal.\"

    But a human expert decides:

    • Is it clinically meaningful?

    • Is there another explanation?

    • Should regulatory action be taken?

    2. What is Pharmacovigilance?

    Before AI, we need to understand PV.

    The science and activities related to:

    • Detecting adverse drug reactions

    • Assessing medicine risks

    • Preventing patient harm

    • Ensuring drug safety

    Example:

    Thousands of patients use it.

    • Liver problems

    • Heart issues

    PV teams analyze these reports.
