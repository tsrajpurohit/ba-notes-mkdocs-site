    \"LLM can summarize clinical documents.\"

    Good.

    But a company asks:

    • How will we measure success?

    PART 1: AI Business Analysis Fundamentals

    1. What is Business Analysis?

    Before AI, understand traditional BA.

    Simple Definition:

    A Business Analyst (BA) is a person who identifies business problems, understands user needs, and converts them into requirements that technology teams can build.

    Think of BA as a translator:

    Business language:

    \"Our safety team spends too much time reviewing adverse event reports.\"

    Technology language:

    \"Build an AI system that extracts adverse events and prioritizes cases.\"

    The BA creates this bridge.

    2. Why Do We Need an AI Business Analyst?

    AI projects are different from normal software projects.

    A normal software project asks:

    \"Can we build this feature?\"

    An AI project asks:

    \"Can AI solve this problem effectively?\"

    Example:

    Business request:

    \"We want an AI chatbot.\"

    A weak approach:

    Start building chatbot.

    AI BA approach:

    Ask:

    Business Questions:

    • Who will use it?

    • What problem does it solve?

    • How much time does it save?

    • What decisions will it support?

    AI Questions:

    • Do we have enough data?

    • Is AI suitable?

    • How accurate should it be?

    • What risks exist?

    Now we learn one of the most important AI BA skills:

    Finding the Right AI Opportunity

    Many companies make this mistake:

    ❌ \"We want to use AI. Where can we apply it?\"

    A good AI BA thinks differently:

    ✅ \"What business problem exists, and can AI solve it?\"

    2. What is an AI Use Case?

    Simple Definition:

    An AI use case is a specific business problem where Artificial Intelligence can create measurable value.

    It includes:

    • Problem

    • Users

    • Data

    • AI capability

    • Expected benefit

    Example:

    Not a Use Case:

    \"We need an AI chatbot.\"

    Why?

    Because it describes technology, not a problem.

    Good AI Use Case:

    \"Medical reviewers spend 4 hours daily summarizing clinical documents. An AI assistant can summarize documents and reduce review time.\"

    9. AI vs Automation Decision

    Very important interview topic.

    Not every problem needs AI.

    Rule-Based Automation

    Use when:

    • Rules are clear

    • Output is predictable

    Example:

    \"If invoice amount > \$10,000, send approval.\"

    No AI needed.

    AI Solution

    Use when:

    • Data is complex

    • Pattern recognition needed

    • Human language involved

    Example:

    Extract adverse events from medical reports.

    Comparison:

    Automation AI

    Fixed rules Learns patterns

    Predictable output Probabilistic output

    Simple decisions Complex decisions

    If/then logic ML/LLM models

    Simple Definition:

    AI Requirement Engineering is the process of identifying, documenting, and managing requirements for an AI-based product or system.

    It defines what the AI system should do, what data it needs, how well it should perform, and what limitations it must follow.

    2. Why AI Requirements Are Different

    Traditional software:

    Example:

    \"When user clicks login button, system should authenticate user.\"

    The output is predictable.

    AI system:

    Example:

    \"AI should identify adverse events from medical reports.\"

    The output is not always 100% predictable.

    AI depends on:

    • Data quality

    • Model capability

    • Context

    • Confidence level

    Therefore AI requirements must define:

    What AI should do

    +

    How accurate it should be

    +

    What data it needs

    +

    How humans interact with it

    +

    What risks are acceptable

    PART 4: AI Feasibility Analysis

    1. Introduction to AI Feasibility Analysis

    In previous parts, we learned:

    Now comes the most important question before building an AI product:

    \"Should we build this AI solution or not?\"

    This is where AI Feasibility Analysis comes in.

    Simple Definition:

    AI Feasibility Analysis is the process of evaluating whether an AI solution is technically possible, financially valuable, operationally practical, and safe to implement.

    In simple words:

    Before spending millions on AI development, we check whether AI is the right solution for the problem.

    2. Why AI Feasibility Analysis is Important

    Many AI projects fail because companies jump directly into development.

    Example:

    Business says:

    \"We need an AI system that predicts patient outcomes.\"

    A team starts building.

    After months:

    Problems:

    • No quality data

    • Poor accuracy

    • Users don\'t trust it

    • Regulatory issues

    A good AI BA asks questions first:

    Is the problem suitable for AI?

    ↓

    Do we have enough data?

    ↓

    Can AI achieve acceptable accuracy?

    ↓

    Is the business value worth the investment?

    ↓

    Are risks manageable?

    Accuracy

    Recall

    How many actual cases did AI find?

    Example:

    100 real cases exist.

    AI finds 95.

    Recall = 95%

    Balance between precision and recall.

    Requirement:

    Documentation explains what AI does, why it exists, what data it uses, how it performs, and how users should trust it.

    • Who approved it?

    ❌ Difficult maintenance\ ❌ Compliance problems\ ❌ Lack of trust\ ❌ Risk of misuse
