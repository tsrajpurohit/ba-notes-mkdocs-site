    Lesson 1 -- Building a Professional BA Portfolio (Industry Standard)

    Learning Objective

    By the end of this lesson, you will understand:

    ✅ What a BA portfolio actually is\ ✅ What recruiters and hiring managers expect\ ✅ How to structure BA case studies\ ✅ Which documents to create\ ✅ How to present yourself as an industry-ready BA\ ✅ How we will build your portfolio step-by-step

    1. What is a BA Portfolio?

    A BA Portfolio is a collection of your practical business analysis work that proves:

    \"I can analyze business problems, understand users, define requirements, and help deliver software solutions.\"

    It is not just a collection of certificates.

    A certificate says:

    \"I learned BA concepts.\"

    A portfolio says:

    \"I can perform BA activities in a real project.\"

    2. What Does an Industry BA Actually Do?

    Imagine a company wants to build an AI Pharmacovigilance System.

    The company does not start with:

    \"Developer, create an AI model.\"

    1. Where Does a BA Enter a Project?

    Many beginners think:

    \"BA receives requirements and writes documents.\"

    That is incomplete.

    A professional BA enters before requirements exist.

    The project starts like this:

    Business Pain

    2. The First Meeting: Discovery Workshop

    A discovery workshop is the first structured conversation between:

    • Business stakeholders

    • Users

    • Product Owner

    • BA

    • Technical team (sometimes)

    Purpose:

    Understand WHY the organization needs a solution before discussing WHAT to build.

    Example Project

    AI Pharmacovigilance Platform

    Stakeholder says:

    \"We need an AI system for pharmacovigilance.\"

    A weak BA immediately asks:

    \"What features do you need?\"

    A strong BA asks:

    \"Why do you need this system?\"

    3. Problem Discovery Framework

    A BA investigates five areas:

    1. Current Situation

    Question:

    \"How is the process working today?\"

    Example:

    Current process:

    Safety Report Received

    ↓

    PV Associate Reads Document

    ↓

    Manual Data Entry

    ↓

    Medical Review

    ↓

    Regulatory Submission

    2. Pain Points

    Question:

    \"What problems are users facing?\"

    Example:

    Pain Point Impact

    Manual data entry Slow processing

    Duplicate cases Extra workload

    Human errors Compliance risk

    Large document volume Backlog

    3. Business Impact

    Question:

    \"Why does this matter to the business?\"

    Example:

    Without improvement:

    • Increased operational cost

    • Delayed regulatory submission

    • Compliance penalties

    • Employee burnout

    4. Business Goal

    Question:

    \"What outcome does the business want?\"

    Example:

    Business Goal:

    \"Reduce safety case processing time by 50%.\"

    5. Success Criteria

    Question:

    \"How will we know the project succeeded?\"

    Example:

    Metric Current Target

    Processing Time 15 days 7 days

    Manual Review 100% 40%

    Error Rate 8% 2%

    4. Problem Statement Creation

    A BA converts discussions into a clear problem statement.

    Bad Problem Statement ❌

    \"We need AI.\"

    Why?

    Because AI is a solution, not the problem.

    Good Problem Statement ✅

    The pharmacovigilance team receives increasing numbers of safety reports, causing delays in case processing and increasing compliance risk due to manual review processes.

    Structure:

    Current Situation

    +

    Problem

    +

    Business Impact

    5. Problem vs Solution

    This is one of the most important BA skills.

    Example:

    Business says:

    \"We need a chatbot.\"

    BA should ask:

    \"Why?\"

    Possible actual problem:

    Patients cannot get quick answers

    |

    ↓

    High call center workload

    |

    ↓

    Need faster information access

    Solution may be:

    • Chatbot

    • Knowledge base

    • Improved FAQ

    • Better communication process

    The BA discovers the real need.

    6. Discovery Workshop Questions

    A BA uses structured questions.

    Business Understanding Questions

    Current Process

    • How does the process work today?

    • Who performs each step?

    • How long does it take?

    • What tools are currently used?

    Problem Questions

    • What is the biggest challenge?

    • Where do delays happen?

    • What mistakes occur?

    • What complaints do users report?

    Impact Questions

    • How does this affect revenue?

    • How does this affect customers?

    • How does this affect compliance?

    Future State Questions

    • What does success look like?

    • What improvement is expected?

    • What should users be able to do?

    Lesson 3 -- Creating Business Case & Project Charter

    BA Professional Level

    Learning Objective

    After this lesson, you will understand:

    ✅ Why every project needs a Business Case\ ✅ Difference between Business Case and Project Charter\ ✅ How a BA creates business justification\ ✅ How to define scope, objectives, risks, assumptions\ ✅ How to create portfolio-ready documents

    1. Why Does a Project Need a Business Case?

    Before an organization spends money, time, and resources, leadership asks:

    \"Why should we invest in this project?\"

    A BA helps answer this question.

    The Business Case proves:

    • What problem exists?

    • Why should we solve it?

    • What benefits will we get?

    • Is the investment worthwhile?

    Example

    Business Problem

    A pharmaceutical company processes:

    • 50,000 safety reports/year

    • Manual review takes 15 days

    • Compliance deadlines are difficult to maintain

    The business asks:

    \"Should we invest in an AI Pharmacovigilance Platform?\"

    The BA creates the Business Case.

    2. Business Case vs Project Charter

    This is a common interview question.

    Business Case

    Answers:

    \"WHY should we do this project?\"

    Focus:

    • Problem

    • Opportunity

    • Benefits

    • Cost justification

    Project Charter

    Answers:

    \"WHAT project are we starting and how will we execute it?\"

    Focus:

    • Scope

    • Objectives

    • Stakeholders

    • Timeline

    • Governance

    Simple Comparison

    Business Case Project Charter

    Why do we need this? What are we going to do?

    Business justification Project authorization

    Created before approval Created after approval

    Focus on value Focus on execution

    PART 1: Creating Business Case

    Project:

    AI Pharmacovigilance Automation Platform

    1. Executive Summary

    Purpose:

    Explain the project in a few sentences.

    Example:

    The pharmaceutical organization receives a growing volume of adverse event reports. Current manual processing creates delays and operational challenges. This project proposes an AI-assisted pharmacovigilance platform to improve case processing efficiency while maintaining human oversight.

    2. Business Problem

    A BA clearly defines the problem.

    Example:

    The pharmacovigilance team relies heavily on manual review and data entry, resulting in slow case processing, increased workload, and potential compliance risks.

    3. Current State Analysis (AS-IS)

    Current process:

    Safety Report Received

    ↓

    Manual Data Extraction

    ↓

    PV Associate Review

    ↓

    Medical Validation

    ↓

    Regulatory Submission

    Problems:

    • High manual effort

    • Slow turnaround

    • Repetitive work

    • Increased error possibility

    4. Proposed Opportunity

    Important:

    Opportunity is not the solution only.

    Example:

    The organization has an opportunity to improve pharmacovigilance operations through intelligent automation, reducing manual effort and improving processing speed.

    5. Proposed Solution

    Now we mention the solution.

    Example:

    An AI-powered platform that can:

    • Extract information from safety reports

    • Identify adverse events

    • Prioritize cases

    • Assist PV reviewers

    6. Business Benefits

    Benefits should be measurable.

    Example:

    Benefit Expected Improvement

    Processing speed 50% faster

    Manual effort 40% reduction

    Data accuracy Improved

    Compliance Better tracking

    7. Cost Consideration

    A BA does not always calculate exact cost but identifies areas.

    Example:

    Costs:

    • AI development

    • Cloud infrastructure

    • Integration

    • Training

    • Maintenance

    8. Risks

    Example:

    Risk Impact Mitigation

    AI incorrect prediction High Human review

    Data privacy issue High Security controls

    User adoption Medium Training

    1. What is a Stakeholder?

    A stakeholder is:

    Any person, group, or organization that can affect the project or is affected by the project.

    A common BA mistake:

    \"Only users are stakeholders.\"

    Wrong.

    A stakeholder can:

    • Approve the project

    • Fund the project

    • Use the system

    • Build the system

    • Validate the system

    • Reject the system

    2. Stakeholder Categories

    1. Business Stakeholders

    People who own the business problem.

    Examples:

    • Head of Pharmacovigilance

    • Drug Safety Director

    • Business Sponsor

    Their concern:

    \"Will this create business value?\"

    2. End Users

    People who use the system daily.

    Examples:

    • PV Scientists

    • Medical Reviewers

    • Case Processors

    Their concern:

    \"Will this make my work easier?\"

    3. Technical Stakeholders

    People who build and maintain the solution.

    Examples:

    • Software Developers

    • AI Engineers

    • Solution Architects

    • Database Engineers

    Their concern:

    \"Can we build this technically?\"

    4. Compliance Stakeholders

    Very important in healthcare.

    Examples:

    • Regulatory Affairs

    • Quality Assurance

    • Data Privacy Team

    Their concern:

    \"Is this safe and compliant?\"

    Lesson 14 -- UAT & Release Management

    Validating the Product Before Business Launch

    Learning Objective

    After this lesson, you will understand:

    ✅ What UAT is and why BAs manage it\ ✅ Difference between QA Testing and UAT\ ✅ How to create UAT plans and test scenarios\ ✅ How business users validate solutions\ ✅ How BAs support release management\ ✅ How to document production readiness

    1. What is UAT?

    UAT = User Acceptance Testing

    UAT answers:

    \"Does this solution actually solve the business problem?\"

    It is performed by:

    • Business users

    • Subject Matter Experts (SMEs)

    • Product Owner

    • Key stakeholders

    Example

    Development team says:

    \"The AI extraction feature works.\"

    Technical testing checks:

    ✅ Code works\ ✅ API works\ ✅ Data is stored

    But business asks:

    \"Can a PV Scientist actually review a safety case faster?\"

    That is UAT.

    2. QA Testing vs UAT

    Common interview question.

    QA Testing UAT

    Checks software quality Checks business suitability

    Performed by QA team Performed by business users

    Finds technical defects Validates business needs

    Focus: Does it work? Focus: Is it useful?

    Example:

    QA Test

    \"Does the upload button accept PDF files?\"

    UAT Test

    \"Can PV Scientists successfully upload and process safety reports as part of their daily workflow?\"
