    Learning Objectives

    By the end of this lesson, you will understand:

    • What an AI Product Manager (AI PM) really does

    • How AI PM differs from a Business Analyst

    First...

    Many people think

    AI Product Manager = Product Manager who uses ChatGPT.

    Wrong.

    Others think

    AI Product Manager builds AI models.

    Also wrong.

    Others think

    AI PM writes Python.

    Not required.

    The AI Product Manager is the person who converts business problems into AI solutions.

    Think of them as the translator between:

    Business ↔ AI Team ↔ Customers

    Imagine Building a Hospital AI

    Hospital CEO says:

    \"Doctors are spending too much time reading adverse event reports.\"

    The AI team says:

    \"We\'ll fine-tune a transformer with RAG and embeddings.\"

    Doctors say:

    \"We don\'t care. Just make it faster.\"

    Who understands all three?

    The AI Product Manager.

    AI PM Responsibilities

    An AI Product Manager owns the entire product journey.

    1. Understand Business Problem

    Example:

    \"Our safety team spends 8 hours/day reviewing reports.\"

    Don\'t jump to AI.

    Ask:

    Why?

    2. Validate Whether AI Is Needed

    Bad AI solution:

    Use GPT because GPT is popular.

    Good AI solution:

    Use AI only if it solves a real problem.

    Sometimes a simple search filter or workflow automation is enough.

    Instead of saying

    \"Build chatbot\"

    AI PM says

    Success means:

    • Response under 5 seconds

    • 90% useful answers

    • Reduce manual effort by 40%

    • Doctors trust recommendations

    • Hallucination rate below target threshold

    AI PM vs Business Analyst

    This is especially important for your career path.

    Business Analyst AI Product Manager

    Understands requirements Defines product vision and strategy

    Documents requirements Decides what to build and why

    Creates user stories Prioritizes the product backlog

    Supports delivery Owns product outcomes

    Works with stakeholders Aligns customers, business, and AI teams

    Focuses on analysis Balances analysis with business decisions

    Think of it this way:

    Business Analyst: \"What do users need?\"

    AI Product Manager: \"What should we build first to create the most value?\"

    A strong BA is often an excellent foundation for becoming an AI PM.

    Healthcare AI Example (Pharmacovigilance)

    Imagine you\'re building an AI Pharmacovigilance Platform.

    Business Problem

    Safety reviewers spend hours reading Individual Case Safety Reports (ICSRs).

    AI PM Responsibilities

    • Interview pharmacovigilance experts.

    • Map the current workflow.

    • Define measurable success (e.g., reduce review time by 50%).

    • Ensure high-quality training data.

    • Prioritize MVP features:

      1. Case summarization

      2. Seriousness classification

      3. Duplicate detection

    • Coordinate with ML engineers and developers.

    • Define evaluation metrics (accuracy, recall, reviewer acceptance).

    • Plan user feedback loops and model monitoring after launch.

    This combines your pharmacology background with Business Analysis and AI product thinking.

    1. How would you identify whether AI is the right solution for a problem?

    2. How would you prioritize AI features for an MVP?

    3. How would you measure the success of an AI product?

    4. What would you do if users lose trust in the AI\'s recommendations?

    5. How would you reduce hallucinations in an LLM-based application?

    6. What metrics would you monitor after launch?

    7. How would you explain an AI decision to a non-technical stakeholder?

    8. How would you balance model accuracy, latency, and operational cost?

    Real Industry Scenario

    A company wants to build an AI assistant for adverse event case processing.

    The CEO says:

    \"We need AI.\"

    As the AI PM, your first questions should be:

    • What specific problem are we solving?

    • How is the work done today?

    • Which users are affected?

    • How much time or cost does the current process consume?

    • What data is available?

    • What does success look like?

    • Are there regulatory or privacy constraints?

    • Is AI actually necessary, or would a simpler automation solve the problem?

    These questions prevent building expensive AI that delivers little value.

    You learned that:

    • AI Product Managers solve business problems using AI---not AI for its own sake.

    • AI PMs connect business, users, engineering, and data science teams.

    • AI products are probabilistic and require continuous monitoring.

    • Success depends on business value, user trust, model quality, and operational performance.

    • AI PMs need a blend of product strategy, AI literacy, analytics, and communication.

    • Your Business Analysis skills are a strong stepping stone toward becoming an AI Product Manager in healthcare AI.

    What is Product Discovery?

    Before writing a single line of code...

    Before training any AI model...

    Before creating wireframes...

    A Product Manager asks:

    \"Should we build this at all?\"

    This is called Product Discovery.

    Product Discovery vs Product Delivery

    Product Discovery Product Delivery

    What problem exists? Build the solution

    Who has the problem? Develop features

    Is it worth solving? Test software

    Is AI needed? Deploy application

    Validate assumptions Release product

    Think of Discovery as deciding the destination, while Delivery is driving the car.

    The Biggest Mistake Companies Make

    CEO:

    \"Everyone is using AI. We need AI too.\"

    Engineering Team:

    \"Let\'s build a chatbot.\"

    Six months later...

    ❌ Nobody uses it.

    Why?

    Because they solved technology, not a business problem.

    Golden Rule of AI Products

    Never start with AI. Start with the problem.

    Wrong:

    We have GPT

    ↓

    Let\'s find somewhere to use it

    Correct:

    We have a business problem

    ↓

    Can AI solve it?

    ↓

    If yes,

    Which AI approach is best?

    The AI Product Discovery Framework

    Professional AI PMs usually follow this sequence:

    Business Problem

    ↓

    Customer Research

    ↓

    Current Process Analysis

    ↓

    Pain Point Identification

    ↓

    Opportunity Analysis

    ↓

    AI Feasibility

    ↓

    Business Value

    ↓

    MVP Definition

    Notice that AI Feasibility comes after understanding the problem, not before.

    Step 1 --- Understand the Business Problem

    Example:

    A Pharmacovigilance company says:

    \"Case processing is too slow.\"

    An inexperienced PM hears:

    \"Need AI summarization.\"

    An experienced AI PM asks:

    • Slow for whom?

    • Which step?

    • How slow?

    • Compared to what?

    • What causes delays?

    The 5 Whys Technique

    Problem:

    Case processing takes 30 minutes.

    Why?

    Because reviewers read long reports.

    Why?

    Because reports are unstructured.

    Why?

    Because data comes from multiple sources.

    Why?

    Because hospitals submit different formats.

    Why?

    Because there is no standardized preprocessing.

    Now you understand the root cause, not just the symptom.

    Discovery often uses Business Analysis techniques like Root Cause Analysis (RCA), which you\'ve already studied.

    Step 2 --- Identify Customer Pain Points

    Every AI opportunity starts with pain.

    Pain usually falls into one of these categories:

    Time

    Example:

    Review takes 40 minutes.

    Opportunity:

    Reduce to 10 minutes.

    Cost

    Example:

    100 reviewers required.

    Opportunity:

    Reduce manual effort.

    Errors

    Example:

    Human reviewers miss duplicate reports.

    Opportunity:

    AI-assisted duplicate detection.

    Scale

    Example:

    Company receives 2 million reports yearly.

    Humans cannot scale.

    AI can assist.

    Decision Support

    Example:

    Doctors struggle to identify serious adverse events quickly.

    AI can prioritize high-risk cases.

    Pain Score Framework

    AI PMs often evaluate each problem using a simple scoring model.

    Question Score (1--5)

    How frequently does it occur?

    How severe is it?

    Business impact?

    Customer frustration?

    Revenue impact?

    Example:

    Duplicate case detection

    Factor Score

    Frequency 5

    Severity 4

    Revenue 3

    Customer Pain 5

    Total 17/20

    Higher score = higher priority.

    Step 3 --- Is AI Actually Needed?

    This is where great AI PMs stand out.

    Not every problem needs AI.

    Consider these scenarios:

    Scenario A

    Need to search patient records.

    A database search works perfectly.

    No AI required.

    Scenario B

    Need to summarize 15-page medical reports.

    AI is appropriate.

    Scenario C

    Need to calculate tax.

    Simple rules.

    No AI.

    Scenario D

    Need to classify adverse event narratives.

    AI is appropriate.

    AI vs Automation Decision Matrix

    Problem Type Automation AI

    Fixed business rules ✅ ❌

    Calculations ✅ ❌

    Structured workflows ✅ ❌

    Pattern recognition ❌ ✅

    Language understanding ❌ ✅

    Image recognition ❌ ✅

    Predictions ❌ ✅

    Recommendations Sometimes ✅

    Step 4 --- Evaluate AI Feasibility

    Even if AI can solve the problem...

    Can your organization solve it?

    Ask:

    Do we have data?

    No data

    ↓

    No AI.

    Is the data clean?

    Garbage in

    ↓

    Garbage out.

    Is there enough data?

    50 records

    ↓

    Probably insufficient for many ML use cases.

    500,000 quality records

    ↓

    Much stronger foundation.

    Can we legally use the data?

    Healthcare products must consider:

    • Patient privacy

    • Data protection regulations

    • Consent

    • Regulatory requirements

    Can users trust AI?

    If the answer is \"No, never,\" then human review may always remain necessary.

    AI Feasibility Checklist

    Question Yes No

    Business problem exists □ □

    Users need solution □ □

    Quality data available □ □

    AI technically possible □ □

    ROI justifies investment □ □

    Legal constraints manageable □ □

    Users willing to adopt □ □

    If several answers are \"No,\" reconsider the initiative.

    Step 5 --- Estimate Business Value

    A Product Manager thinks beyond technology.

    Estimate:

    Time Saved

    Current review:

    12 minutes

    18 minutes per case.

    10 reviewers

    Customer Satisfaction

    Does AI reduce frustration?

    Competitive Advantage

    Will competitors struggle to match this capability?

    Introduction

    Many people confuse these three concepts:

    • Product Vision

    • Product Strategy

    • Product Roadmap

    They are not the same.

    Think of planning a trip.

    • Vision = Where do you want to go?

    • Strategy = Which route will you take?

    • Roadmap = What are the stops along the way?

    Example:

    Vision:

    Become India\'s best AI Pharmacovigilance platform.

    Strategy:

    Reduce safety review time using Generative AI and predictive analytics.

    Roadmap:

    Release summarization → Duplicate detection → Signal detection → Regulatory writing.

    The Product Vision

    A Product Vision answers one question:

    \"What future are we trying to create?\"

    It is not a feature list.

    It is not a project plan.

    It is the long-term destination.

    Good Vision Example

    \"Empower every drug safety professional to review adverse event cases in minutes instead of hours using trusted AI.\"

    Notice:

    • Clear

    • Inspiring

    • Customer-focused

    • Outcome-oriented

    Bad Vision Example

    \"Build an AI chatbot.\"

    This describes a feature, not a vision.

    Characteristics of a Great Vision

    A strong vision is:

    • Inspiring

    • Easy to understand

    • Customer-centric

    • Long-term (3--10 years)

    • Independent of technology

    Technology changes.

    Vision should remain stable.

    Product Strategy

    Strategy answers:

    \"How will we achieve our vision?\"

    It defines:

    • Target customers

    • Problems to solve

    • Competitive advantage

    • Business goals

    • Success metrics

    Example

    Vision:

    AI-assisted pharmacovigilance for every global pharmaceutical company.

    Strategy:

    • Target medium and large pharma companies.

    • Focus on case processing first.

    • Use Generative AI to reduce manual work.

    • Integrate with existing safety databases.

    • Build trust through explainable AI.

    Product Roadmap

    The roadmap answers:

    \"What will we build over time?\"

    Example:

    Quarter 1

    • AI Case Summary

    • Smart Search

    Quarter 2

    • Duplicate Detection

    • MedDRA Suggestions

    Quarter 3

    • Seriousness Classification

    • Workflow Automation

    Quarter 4

    • Signal Detection

    • Regulatory Drafting

    Notice:

    The roadmap changes frequently.

    The vision rarely changes.

    ↓

    Professional Product Managers start with vision.

    • Increase revenue

    Business Goal:

    Reduce operational costs by 30%.

    AI Strategy:

    Automate repetitive case summarization.

    AI-generated summaries.

    \"Why should customers choose this product?\"

    Who have

    (Problem)

    Our Product

    (Solution)

    Unlike

    Drug safety reviewers

    Positioning defines how customers perceive your product.

    Example:

    \"We are the AI Copilot for Pharmacovigilance Teams.\"

    Not:

    Positioning should be simple and memorable.

    • Where are they weak?

    • What do customers complain about?

    Introduction

    Having a great AI product idea is not enough.

    Engineers need clarity.

    Data Scientists need clarity.

    Designers need clarity.

    Business stakeholders need clarity.

    That clarity comes from a Product Requirements Document (PRD).

    Think of a PRD as the blueprint of the product.

    Just like an architect creates a blueprint before constructing a building, an AI Product Manager creates a PRD before development begins.

    What is a PRD?

    A Product Requirements Document (PRD) describes:

    • What we are building

    • Why we are building it

    • Who it is for

    • What success looks like

    • What is in scope

    • What is out of scope

    It focuses on the product, not the implementation.

    PRD vs BRD vs FRD

    You\'ve already studied BRD and FRD as a Business Analyst.

    Now let\'s compare them.

    Document Owner Purpose

    BRD (Business Business Business needs and objectives Requirements Document) Analyst

    FRD (Functional BA / System Functional system behavior Requirements Document) Analyst

    PRD (Product Requirements Product Manager Product vision, features, Document) / AI PM priorities, and success metrics

    Simple Analogy

    Imagine building a hospital.

    BRD

    Why do we need a hospital?

    FRD

    How should each department function?

    PRD

    What hospital should we build first, who is it for, and what outcomes should it deliver?

    Traditional PRD vs AI PRD

    Traditional software:

    Feature:

    Login System

    Requirement:

    User enters username and password.

    Output:

    Login successful.

    Always deterministic.

    AI Product:

    Feature:

    Medical Report Summarization

    Requirement:

    AI generates a concise summary.

    Possible outputs:

    Summary A

    Summary B

    Summary C

    All may be correct.

    AI products are probabilistic, not deterministic.

    Section 1 --- Product Overview

    Product Name

    • High operational cost

    • Slow processing

    • Reviewer fatigue

    • Delayed case completion

    We describe the problem, not the solution.

    Bad Goal:

    Improve AI.

    Good Goals:

    • Reduce review time by 40%.

    • Improve reviewer productivity.

    • Maintain regulatory compliance.

    • Increase user satisfaction.

    Every goal should be:

    • Specific

    • Measurable

    • Achievable

    • Relevant

    • Time-bound

    Example:

    Reduce average case review time from 25 minutes to 15 minutes within six months of launch.

    Example Personas:

    Primary

    Drug Safety Reviewer

    • Faster reviews

    Safety Manager

    Needs:

    • Team productivity

    • Quality monitoring

    • Compliance reporting

    Admin

    Needs:

    • User management

    • Audit logs

    • System configuration

    Section 5 --- Functional Requirements

    Functional requirements define what the system should do.

    Example:

    FR-1

    The system shall allow users to upload an Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR).

    FR-2

    The system shall generate an AI summary.

    FR-3

    Users shall be able to edit the AI summary.

    FR-4

    Users shall approve or reject the summary.

    FR-5

    The system shall store user feedback for future model improvement.

    Section 6 --- AI Requirements

    This section is unique to AI products.

    Example:

    The AI shall:

    • Summarize reports in plain medical language.

    • Preserve clinically important information.

    • Avoid inventing facts (hallucinations).

    • Highlight uncertainty when confidence is low.

    • Reference source sections where possible.

    • Support human review before final approval.

    Unlike normal software, AI requirements include quality expectations, not just functionality.

    Section 7 --- Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs)

    AI systems must also satisfy operational requirements.

    Performance

    • Response time under 5 seconds.

    Availability

    • 99.9% uptime.

    Security

    • Encryption in transit and at rest.

    Privacy

    • Protect patient information.

    Explainability

    • Show why the AI produced its answer.

    Scalability

    • Process thousands of reports daily.

    Reliability

    • Continue functioning during peak loads.

    AI-Specific NFRs

    These are often missing in beginner PRDs.

    Include items such as:

    • Hallucination rate below defined threshold.

    • Confidence score displayed.

    • Human review required for high-risk outputs.

    • Prompt and response logging for audits.

    • Model version tracking.

    • Bias monitoring.

    • Drift monitoring.

    • Safe fallback behavior if AI fails.

    As a user,\ I want to upload a document,\ so that I can save it.

    As a Drug Safety Reviewer,\ I want AI to summarize an adverse event report,\ so that I can review cases faster while maintaining accuracy.

    The story focuses on business value, not technology.

    Story 2

    I want AI to identify duplicate cases,

    Story 3

    As a Safety Manager,

    I want dashboards showing AI performance,

    so that I can monitor quality and adoption.

    AI Acceptance Criteria

    Acceptance criteria define how we know the feature is acceptable.

    Traditional Example:

    • User clicks Login.

    • Login succeeds.

    Easy.

    AI Example:

    Much harder.

    Example

    Feature:

    Acceptance Criteria:

    • Summary generated within 5 seconds.

    • Allows manual editing.

    • Shows confidence score.

    • Provides access to the source document.

    • Stores reviewer feedback.

    We don\'t require 100% identical wording.

    We require the summary to meet quality expectations.

    What is an LLM?

    LLM = Large Language Model

    Examples:

    • ChatGPT

    • Claude

    • Gemini

    • Llama

    • Mistral

    An LLM predicts the next most likely token (word or sub-word piece) based on context.

    It does not \"think\" like a human.

    It predicts statistically likely text.

    Simplified Example

    Input:

    The capital of France is...

    The model predicts:

    Paris.

    Because it has learned language patterns from massive datasets.

    Important Misconception

    People think:

    LLM = Database.

    Wrong.

    LLMs don\'t reliably \"store\" your company\'s latest information.

    They generate responses from learned patterns.

    That\'s why enterprises use RAG.

    Tokens

    LLMs don\'t read words.

    They read tokens.

    Example:

    Sentence:

    I love pharmacology.

    Might become:

    I

    love

    pharma

    cology

    Every AI model has a context window, measured in tokens.

    Why Tokens Matter

    Long documents consume more tokens.

    More tokens mean:

    • Higher cost

    • Slower responses

    • Context limits

    As an AI PM, you\'ll often balance:

    • Accuracy

    • Speed

    • Cost

    Context Window

    The context window is the amount of information the model can consider in a single request.

    Example:

    Prompt

    +

    Conversation History

    +

    Documents

    +

    Instructions

    ↓

    LLM

    If the context exceeds the limit:

    Older information may be removed or ignored, depending on the implementation.

    Prompt Engineering

    A prompt is simply an instruction given to the model.

    Weak prompt:

    Summarize.

    Better prompt:

    Summarize this adverse event report in under 150 words. Include:

    • Patient demographics

    • Suspect drug

    • Adverse event

    • Seriousness

    • Outcome

    Specific prompts generally produce more consistent results.

    System Prompt vs User Prompt

    System Prompt

    Defines the AI\'s role and behavior.

    Example:

    You are a pharmacovigilance assistant. Never invent medical facts. If information is missing, explicitly state that it is unavailable.

    User Prompt

    Changes with each request.

    Example:

    Summarize this case report.

    What are Embeddings?

    This concept is essential.

    Computers don\'t naturally understand meaning.

    Embeddings convert text into numerical vectors that capture semantic similarity.

    Imagine:

    \"Headache\"

    and

    \"Migraine\"

    They use different words.

    But their embeddings are close because their meanings are related.

    Simple Analogy

    Imagine every document is represented as a point on a giant map.

    Similar documents appear close together.

    Different topics appear farther apart.

    Embeddings create this map.

    Vector Database

    A normal database searches for exact matches.

    Example:

    Search:

    \"Paracetamol\"

    Finds:

    Paracetamol

    But not necessarily:

    Acetaminophen (depending on the data).

    A vector database searches by meaning.

    Search:

    \"Pain medicine\"

    Returns:

    • Paracetamol

    • Ibuprofen

    • Acetaminophen

    because they are semantically related.

    Popular vector databases include:

    • Pinecone

    • Weaviate

    • Milvus

    • Chroma

    • Qdrant

    • pgvector (PostgreSQL extension)

    As an AI PM, you don\'t need to administer them, but you should know why they are used.

    RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

    This is one of the most important AI architectures.

    Without RAG:

    User Question

    ↓

    LLM

    ↓

    Answer

    Problem:

    The answer may be outdated or hallucinated.

    With RAG:

    User Question

    ↓

    Embedding

    ↓

    Vector Search

    ↓

    Relevant Documents

    ↓

    LLM

    ↓

    Grounded Answer

    The LLM answers using retrieved company documents, not just its general knowledge.

    Why Enterprises Love RAG

    Imagine asking:

    \"What is our company\'s SOP for processing serious adverse events?\"

    A general LLM doesn\'t know your internal SOP.

    With RAG:

    • It searches your SOP documents.

    • Retrieves the relevant sections.

    • Generates an answer based on those documents.

    Benefits:

    • More accurate.

    • Easier to update knowledge.

    • Better traceability.

    • Reduced hallucinations.

    A chatbot answers questions.

    An AI Agent performs tasks.

    • Generates the summary.

    • Creates the Jira ticket.

    ↓

    MCP (Model Context Protocol)

    As AI systems become more complex, they need a standard way to connect with external tools and data sources.

    Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open protocol that allows AI applications to interact with tools, databases, documents, and services in a consistent way.

    Think of it as:

    \"USB-C for AI applications.\"

    Instead of building a custom integration for every tool, MCP provides a standardized interface.

    As an AI PM, you don\'t need to implement MCP, but you\'ll increasingly encounter it in enterprise AI architectures.

    Memory

    Many AI assistants use memory.

    Without memory:

    Every conversation starts from zero.

    With memory:

    The AI remembers relevant long-term preferences or project context (subject to user controls and privacy settings).

    Examples:

    • Preferred writing style.

    • Frequently used terminology.

    • Product preferences.

    • Ongoing project context.

    Enterprise memory requires careful governance, privacy, and user control.

    Guardrails

    AI should not answer everything.

    Guardrails are rules that constrain AI behavior.

    Examples:

    • Never reveal confidential information.

    • Never fabricate medical facts.

    • Refuse unsafe requests.

    • Mask sensitive patient identifiers.

    • Require citations for regulatory content.

    • Escalate uncertain cases to a human.

    Guardrails are a product requirement---not just a technical detail.

    Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

    In high-risk industries like healthcare, AI should assist---not replace---experts.

    Example workflow:

    ICSR Upload

    ↓

    AI Summary

    ↓

    Human Review

    ↓

    Approve / Edit

    ↓

    Final Record

    This balances efficiency with safety and accountability.

    Accuracy

    Formula:

    Correct Predictions

    -------------------

    Total Predictions

    Example:

    100 reports classified.

    Correct:

    92

    Accuracy:

    92%

    Simple.

    But...

    Accuracy alone can be misleading.

    Why Accuracy Can Mislead

    Imagine:

    1000 reports.

    990 are normal.

    10 are serious.

    Model predicts:

    Everything is normal.

    Accuracy:

    990/1000 = 99%

    Amazing?

    No.

    It missed every serious case.

    Healthcare cannot rely on accuracy alone.

    Precision

    Precision answers:

    \"When the AI predicts Positive, how often is it correct?\"

    Formula:

    True Positives

    ----------------------------

    True Positives + False Positives

    Example:

    AI predicts:

    100 serious cases.

    Actually serious:

    95

    False alarms:

    5

    Precision:

    95%

    High precision = fewer false alarms.

    Recall

    Recall answers:

    \"Did the AI find all the real positive cases?\"

    Formula:

    True Positives

    ----------------------------

    True Positives + False Negatives

    Example:

    100 serious cases exist.

    AI detects:

    90

    Misses:

    10

    Recall:

    90%

    High recall = fewer missed cases.

    Precision vs Recall

    Imagine airport security.

    High Precision:

    Only suspicious passengers are stopped.

    Very few innocent people are delayed.

    High Recall:

    Almost every dangerous passenger is caught.

    But more innocent passengers are also checked.

    Trade-off.

    Which Matters More?

    Depends on the business problem.

    Use Case More Important

    Spam detection Precision

    Cancer diagnosis Recall

    Fraud detection Recall

    Marketing recommendations Precision

    Pharmacovigilance signal Usually Recall (while maintaining detection acceptable precision)

    An AI PM must understand these trade-offs.

    F1 Score

    F1 balances Precision and Recall.

    Think of it as:

    Overall classification quality when both precision and recall matter.

    Instead of memorizing the formula, remember:

    High Precision + High Recall = High F1.

    Confusion Matrix

    Every AI PM should understand this diagram.

    Actual

    Positive Negative

    Predicted

    Positive TP FP

    Negative FN TN

    Where:

    TP = True Positive

    FP = False Positive

    FN = False Negative

    TN = True Negative

    Almost every AI interview discusses this.

    1. What is Responsible AI?

    Responsible AI means:

    Designing, developing, deploying, and managing AI systems in a way that is safe, fair, transparent, and accountable.

    Users should understand:

    \"Why did AI make this recommendation?\"

    4. Accountability

    Someone must be responsible for AI outcomes.

    \"The model decided.\"

    A human organization remains accountable.

    5. Privacy

    Sensitive information must be protected.

    Especially:

    • Patient data

    • Medical records

    • Personal identifiers

    6. Safety

    AI should minimize harmful outcomes.

    Examples:

    • Detect uncertainty

    • Escalate risky cases

    4. AI Bias

    Bias occurs when AI produces systematically unfair outcomes.

    Mostly from one population.

    Problem:

    Training data does not represent reality.

    Example:

    Too few examples from certain groups.

    2. Algorithm Bias

    The model learns patterns that create unfair outcomes.

    3. Human Bias

    People may unknowingly create biased:

    • Labels

    • Requirements

    • Evaluation criteria

    Bias Example in Healthcare AI

    Only from one country\'s hospitals.

    Performance decreases.

    The data does not represent global patients.
