    MODULE 10 -- PRODUCT MANAGEMENT IN AGILE

    Lesson 1: Product Management Fundamentals

    1. What is Product Management?

    Product Management is the discipline of:

    Identifying customer problems, creating solutions, delivering value, and continuously improving the product.

    A Product Manager (PM) acts as the connection between:

    Customers

    ↓

    Business Goals

    ↓

    Product Strategy

    ↓

    Development Team

    ↓

    Product Release

    ↓

    Customer Feedback

    A Product Manager asks:

    • What problem should we solve?

    • Who are we solving it for?

    • Why is this important?

    • What should we build?

    • How will we measure success?

    2. What is a Product?

    A product is anything that provides value to users.

    It can be:

    Physical Product

    Example:

    • Smartphone

    • Medical device

    Digital Product

    Example:

    • Mobile application

    • SaaS platform

    • Healthcare software

    AI Product

    Example:

    AI Pharmacovigilance Platform:

    A system that helps safety teams process adverse event cases faster using AI.

    3. Product vs Project

    A common interview question.

    Project

    A temporary effort to deliver something.

    Example:

    \"Build a hospital management application in 6 months.\"

    Focus:

    • Timeline

    • Budget

    • Scope

    Product

    A continuous journey of creating value.

    Example:

    \"Continuously improve hospital management software based on user needs.\"

    Focus:

    • Users

    • Value

    • Growth

    • Improvement

    Comparison:

    Project Product

    Temporary Continuous

    Has end date Evolves continuously

    Deliver output Deliver value

    Success = completion Success = user/business impact

    4. Why Does Product Management Exist?

    Without Product Management:

    Companies may build:

    ❌ Features nobody needs\ ❌ Products without market demand\ ❌ Solutions without clear goals

    Product Management ensures:

    ✅ Build the right product\ ✅ For the right users\ ✅ At the right time\ ✅ With measurable outcomes

    5. Role of a Product Manager

    A Product Manager is responsible for product success.

    A PM does not usually:

    ❌ Write all code\ ❌ Design every screen\ ❌ Manage every task

    Instead, PM:

    ✅ Defines direction\ ✅ Understands users\ ✅ Prioritizes problems\ ✅ Makes product decisions\ ✅ Aligns teams

    6. Product Manager Responsibilities

    A PM\'s work can be divided into five areas.

    Area 1: Product Vision

    Question:

    \"Where do we want this product to go?\"

    Example:

    Vision:

    \"Create the world\'s most intelligent pharmacovigilance assistant.\"

    Area 2: Product Strategy

    Question:

    \"How will we achieve that vision?\"

    Example:

    Strategy:

    • Start with AI case extraction

    • Add automation gradually

    • Expand into predictive safety analytics

    Area 3: Discovery

    Question:

    \"What problems should we solve?\"

    Activities:

    • Customer interviews

    • Market research

    • User feedback

    • Competitor analysis

    Area 4: Delivery

    Question:

    \"How do we build and launch it?\"

    Activities:

    • Create roadmap

    • Prioritize features

    • Work with engineering

    • Manage releases

    Area 5: Measurement

    Question:

    \"Did we create value?\"

    Activities:

    • Analyze metrics

    • Track KPIs

    • Improve product

    8. Product Manager vs Business Analyst vs Product Owner

    Very important for your career path.

    Role Main Focus

    Business Analyst Understand and document business needs

    Product Owner Manage Agile backlog and maximize sprint value

    Product Manager Define product vision, strategy, and success

    Business Analyst

    Question:

    \"What does the business need?\"

    Example:

    \"Safety associates need faster case creation.\"

    Product Owner

    Question:

    \"What should the team build next?\"

    Example:

    \"AI extraction feature should be prioritized in Sprint 3.\"

    Product Manager

    Question:

    \"Why should we build this and what outcome do we want?\"

    Example:

    \"Reducing case processing time will improve regulatory compliance and customer value.\"

    • Collecting feedback

    • More users

    • Market expansion

    Stage 4: Maturity

    Focus:

    • Optimization

    • Retention

    • Efficiency

    Stage 5: Decline or Transformation

    Options:

    • Improve product

    • Change strategy

    • Replace technology

    11. Product Thinking vs Feature Thinking

    A beginner thinks:

    \"What feature should we build?\"

    A Product Manager thinks:

    \"What problem should we solve?\"

    Example:

    Customer says:

    \"I need a chatbot.\"

    Feature thinking:

    Build chatbot.

    Product thinking:

    Why?

    Customer:

    \"I cannot find safety information quickly.\"

    Possible solutions:

    • Better search

    • Knowledge base

    • AI assistant

    Before building anything, ask:

    1. Is there a real problem?

    Who has this problem?

    2. Is it valuable?

    Does solving it create impact?

    3. Is it feasible?

    Can we build it?

    4. How will we measure success?

    What metric improves?

    Lesson 2: Product Vision

    1. What is Product Vision?

    A Product Vision describes the future state that a product wants to create.

    It answers:

    \"What future are we trying to build, and why does this product matter?\"

    A product vision gives direction to:

    • Product team

    • Engineering team

    • Design team

    • Business stakeholders

    • Customers

    Without a clear vision:

    Teams may build many features but lose sight of the real purpose.

    Example:

    Bad vision:

    \"Build an AI dashboard.\"

    This describes a feature, not a vision.

    Good vision:

    \"Help pharmaceutical safety teams detect and manage drug risks faster through intelligent AI-powered solutions.\"

    This describes:

    • Who benefits

    • What problem is solved

    • What future we want

    2. Why Product Vision Matters

    A strong vision helps:

    1. Align Teams

    Everyone understands:

    • Why we are building

    • Who we are helping

    • What success looks like

    2. Make Better Decisions

    When a new feature request comes:

    Question:

    \"Does this move us closer to our vision?\"

    If yes:

    Consider building.

    If no:

    Avoid distraction.

    3. Prioritize Better

    Limited resources require choices.

    Vision helps decide:

    Feature A:

    Improves patient safety.

    Feature B:

    Only improves visual design.

    Vision helps choose A.

    3. Product Vision vs Product Mission

    These are often confused.

    Product Vision

    Focus:

    Future destination

    Question:

    \"Where do we want to go?\"

    Example:

    \"Create a world where medication safety monitoring is proactive and intelligent.\"

    Product Mission

    Focus:

    Current purpose

    Question:

    \"What do we do every day to reach that future?\"

    Example:

    \"We help pharmaceutical companies analyze safety data using AI automation.\"

    Comparison:

    Product Vision Product Mission

    Future state Current purpose

    Long-term direction Daily activity

    Inspirational Practical

    4. Product Vision vs Product Strategy

    Another common confusion.

    Vision

    Where?

    Example:

    \"Make drug safety monitoring intelligent.\"

    Strategy

    How?

    Example:

    \"Start with AI case processing, then expand into predictive safety analytics.\"

    Roadmap

    When?

    Example:

    2026:

    AI extraction

    2027:

    Signal detection

    2028:

    Predictive analytics

    Relationship:

    Vision

    Where are we going?

    ↓

    Strategy

    How will we get there?

    ↓

    Roadmap

    When will we deliver?

    5. Elements of a Strong Product Vision

    A good product vision includes:

    1. Target User

    Who are we helping?

    Example:

    Pharmacovigilance professionals

    2. User Problem

    What problem exists?

    Example:

    Manual safety case processing is slow.

    3. Solution Direction

    What change will we create?

    Example:

    AI-assisted safety intelligence.

    4. Future Impact

    Why does it matter?

    Example:

    Faster detection of medicine risks.

    Product Vision Formula

    A simple framework:

    For [target users]\ who struggle with [problem],\ our product provides [solution]\ so that [desired impact].

    Example:

    For pharmaceutical safety teams who struggle with manual case processing, our AI platform provides intelligent automation so that medicine risks can be identified faster and more accurately.

    Example:

    HOW

    8. Creating Product Vision for AI Products

    AI products need special consideration.

    A weak AI vision:

    \"Use AI everywhere.\"

    Problem:

    AI is a technology, not a purpose.

    \"Use AI to reduce repetitive healthcare workflows and enable professionals to make faster, safer decisions.\"

    AI Vision Questions:

    Problem

    Is AI solving a meaningful problem?

    Data

    Do we have quality data?

    Trust

    Will users trust AI output?

    Human Role

    Where is human judgment required?

    Lesson 3: Product Strategy

    1. What is Product Strategy?

    A Product Strategy defines how a company will achieve its product vision.

    It answers:

    \"How will we win in the market and create value for users and the business?\"

    Product Vision:

    \"Where do we want to go?\"

    Product Strategy:

    \"How will we get there?\"

    Example:

    Vision:

    \"Transform drug safety monitoring using AI.\"

    Strategy:

    1. Start with AI-assisted case processing

    2. Reduce manual workload

    3. Build trust with healthcare professionals

    4. Expand into predictive safety intelligence

    2. Why Product Strategy Matters

    Without strategy:

    Teams may build:

    ❌ Too many features\ ❌ Features without users\ ❌ Products without differentiation

    A strong strategy helps:

    ✅ Decide what to build\ ✅ Decide what NOT to build\ ✅ Allocate resources\ ✅ Compete effectively\ ✅ Measure progress

    3. Product Strategy Framework

    A simple product strategy has five components:

    1. Market Understanding

    ↓

    2. Target Users

    ↓

    3. Problem Definition

    ↓

    4. Solution Approach

    ↓

    5. Business Goals

    4. Component 1: Market Understanding

    Before building, a PM studies:

    • Industry trends

    • Customer needs

    • Competitors

    • Market opportunities

    Example:

    AI Pharmacovigilance Market:

    Observation:

    Pharma companies face:

    • Increasing safety data

    • Regulatory pressure

    • High operational costs

    Opportunity:

    AI automation for safety workflows.

    5. Component 2: Target Customers

    A product cannot serve everyone.

    A PM defines:

    Who is the primary customer?

    Example:

    AI PV Platform:

    Primary users:

    • Safety Associates

    • PV Managers

    Secondary users:

    • Medical Reviewers

    • Regulatory Teams

    Customer Segmentation

    Customers can be divided by:

    Industry

    Example:

    • Pharmaceutical companies

    • CROs

    • Hospitals

    Company Size

    Example:

    • Large pharma

    • Mid-size biotech

    • Startups

    User Role

    Example:

    • Analyst

    • Manager

    • Executive

    6. Component 3: Problem Definition

    Great products solve painful problems.

    A PM asks:

    • What problem exists?

    • How severe is it?

    • How often does it happen?

    • Who experiences it?

    Example:

    Problem:

    Safety associates spend hours manually extracting case information.

    Impact:

    • Slow processing

    • Higher workload

    • Reporting delays

    7. Component 4: Solution Strategy

    A PM decides:

    \"What is the best approach?\"

    Important:

    Solution is not always a feature.

    Example:

    User problem:

    \"Finding information takes too long.\"

    Possible solutions:

    Option 1:

    Search improvement

    Option 2:

    AI assistant

    Option 3:

    Better documentation

    PM evaluates:

    • User value

    • Cost

    • Feasibility

    8. Component 5: Business Goals

    A product must create business value.

    Examples:

    Business goals:

    • Increase revenue

    • Reduce operational cost

    • Improve customer retention

    • Enter new markets

    AI PV Platform Example:

    Business Goal:

    Reduce customer processing cost by 30%.

    9. Product Strategy vs Product Roadmap

    Very common interview topic.

    Product Strategy

    Defines:

    WHY and HOW

    Example:

    \"Become the leading AI safety automation platform.\"

    Product Roadmap

    Defines:

    WHEN

    Example:

    Q1:

    Case automation

    Q2:

    AI coding

    Q3:

    Analytics

    Relationship:

    Vision

    ↓

    Strategy

    ↓

    Roadmap

    ↓

    Features

    10. Product-Market Fit (PMF)

    One of the most important product concepts.

    Product-market fit means:

    The product solves a real problem for a specific market, and users find enough value to continue using it.

    Signs of Product-Market Fit:

    ✅ Users actively use product\ ✅ Customers recommend it\ ✅ Demand increases\ ✅ Retention is high

    Example:

    AI PV Platform achieves PMF when:

    • Safety teams depend on it daily

    • Processing time reduces significantly

    • Companies renew subscriptions

    11. Value Proposition

    A value proposition explains:

    Why should customers choose this product?

    Formula:

    For [customer]

    Who needs [problem solved]

    Our product provides [solution]

    Unlike [alternative]

    We deliver [unique benefit]

    Example:

    For pharmaceutical safety teams who struggle with manual case processing, our AI platform provides intelligent automation that reduces processing time while maintaining regulatory compliance.

    Strength:

    Less AI automation

    AI-powered workflow assistance.

    Lesson 4: Market Research and Competitive Analysis

    1. What is Market Research?

    Market Research is the process of collecting and analyzing information about:

    • Customers

    • Industry

    • Competitors

    • Market trends

    • Business opportunities

    A Product Manager uses market research to answer:

    \"Is this the right problem to solve, and is there a market that needs our solution?\"

    Without research, companies may build:

    ❌ Products nobody wants\ ❌ Features users do not need\ ❌ Solutions that cannot compete

    2. Why Market Research is Important for Product Managers

    Market research helps PMs:

    1. Understand Customer Problems

    Example:

    A pharma company says:

    \"We need AI.\"

    A PM investigates:

    Actual problem:

    \"Safety associates spend 70% of their time manually reviewing reports.\"

    The real opportunity is workflow automation.

    2. Identify Market Opportunities

    Example:

    Observation:

    • Regulatory workload increasing

    • Safety data increasing

    • AI adoption growing

    Opportunity:

    AI-powered pharmacovigilance automation.

    3. Make Better Product Decisions

    Instead of:

    \"I think users need this.\"

    PM says:

    \"Research shows 80% of users face this problem.\"

    3. Types of Market Research

    Market research is divided into two categories:

    Primary Research

    +

    Secondary Research

    4. Primary Research

    Primary research means collecting new information directly from users.

    Sources:

    • Customer interviews

    • Surveys

    • User observation

    • Focus groups

    • Feedback sessions

    Example:

    Interview 20 safety associates.

    Questions:

    • What tasks take most time?

    • What problems occur daily?

    • What tools do you currently use?

    Advantages

    ✅ Real customer insights\ ✅ Understand user pain points\ ✅ Discover unmet needs

    Limitations

    ❌ Time consuming\ ❌ Small sample size

    5. Secondary Research

    Secondary research uses existing information.

    Sources:

    • Industry reports

    • Research papers

    • Competitor websites

    • Market analysis

    • Regulatory publications

    Example:

    Researching:

    • Growth of AI in healthcare

    • Pharma technology trends

    • Regulatory technology adoption

    Advantages

    ✅ Faster\ ✅ Large amount of information

    Limitations

    ❌ May not answer specific user problems

    6. Market Research Process

    A PM follows a structured process:

    Define Research Goal

    ↓

    Identify Target Users

    ↓

    Collect Data

    Lesson 7: MVP (Minimum Viable Product)

    1. What is MVP?

    MVP (Minimum Viable Product) is the simplest version of a product that:

    1. Solves a real user problem

    2. Provides enough value for early users

    3. Allows the team to learn from feedback

    Simple definition:

    MVP is the smallest product version that can be released to validate whether the idea solves a real customer problem.

    A common misunderstanding:

    MVP does not mean:

    ❌ Poor quality product\ ❌ Incomplete product\ ❌ A product with only minimum features

    MVP means:

    ✅ Minimum effort\ ✅ Maximum learning\ ✅ Real customer feedback

    2. Why Do We Need MVP?

    Building a complete product is expensive.

    Before investing heavily, companies need to validate:

    • Is this problem real?

    • Do users need this solution?

    • Will customers use it?

    • Will customers pay for it?

    Without MVP:

    Company spends:

    ₹5 crore

    18 months development

    Then discovers:

    Nobody needs the product.

    With MVP:

    Company tests the idea early.

    3. MVP Mindset

    Traditional thinking:

    \"Let\'s build everything and launch.\"

    MVP thinking:

    \"Let\'s build the smallest valuable solution, learn, and improve.\"

    Product cycle:

    Build

    ↓

    Measure

    ↓

    Learn

    ↓

    Improve

    ↓

    Repeat

    4. MVP vs Prototype vs Proof of Concept

    Very important interview topic.

    Prototype

    Purpose:

    Test design or user experience.

    Question:

    \"Can users understand this?\"

    Example:

    Clickable Figma design of AI dashboard.

    Proof of Concept (POC)

    Purpose:

    Test technical feasibility.

    Question:

    \"Can we build this technology?\"

    Example:

    Testing whether AI can extract drug names from documents.

    MVP

    Purpose:

    Test real customer value.

    Question:

    \"Will users use this solution?\"

    Example:

    Working AI extraction tool used by safety associates.

    Comparison:

            **Prototype**         **POC**            **MVP**

    Goal Test idea/design Test technology Test market value

    Users Internal/test users Technical team Real customers

    Output Mockup Experiment Working product

    6. RICE Scoring Framework ⭐

    Very popular in product companies.

    RICE means:

    R - Reach

    How many users will benefit?

    Example:

    1000 users/month

    I - Impact

    How much impact?

    Scale:

    Low → High

    C - Confidence

    How confident are we?

    Example:

    Based on customer research.

    E - Effort

    How much work required?

    Formula:

    RICE Score =

    (Reach × Impact × Confidence)

    ÷

    Effort

    Example:

    Feature:

    AI case extraction

    Reach:

    1000 users

    Impact:

    3

    Confidence:

    80%

    Effort:

    5

    Score:

    (1000 × 3 × 0.8) ÷ 5

    =

    480

    Higher score = Higher priority.

    Lesson 12: A/B Testing and Experimentation

    1. What is A/B Testing?

    A/B Testing is a method of comparing two versions of a product, feature, or experience to determine which one performs better.

    Simple definition:

    A/B testing allows Product Managers to make decisions based on user behavior instead of assumptions.

    Example:

    You have two versions of an AI case review screen:

    Version A:

    • Current design

    Version B:

    • New AI-assisted design

    Users are randomly divided into two groups.

    The PM measures which version creates better results.

    2. Why Do Product Teams Run Experiments?

    Product decisions often involve uncertainty.

    Example:

    Question:

    \"Will adding AI suggestions improve reviewer efficiency?\"

    Instead of guessing:

    Run an experiment.

    A/B testing helps:

    ✅ Validate ideas\ ✅ Reduce product risk\ ✅ Improve user experience\ ✅ Increase adoption\ ✅ Make data-driven decisions

    3. Experimentation Mindset

    Traditional approach:

    \"I believe this feature will work.\"

    Product approach:

    \"Let\'s test whether this feature creates measurable improvement.\"

    The cycle:

    Hypothesis

    ↓

    Experiment Design

    ↓

    Release Test

    ↓

    Collect Data

    ↓

    Analyze Results

    ↓

    Decision

    4. A/B Testing Terminology

    Control Group (A)

    The existing version.

    Also called:

    Baseline.

    Example:

    Current case review workflow.

    Variant Group (B)

    The new version being tested.

    Example:

    AI-powered review workflow.

    Conversion Metric

    The measurement used to evaluate success.

    Example:

    • Completion rate

    • Time saved

    • User adoption

    5. A/B Testing Process

    Step 1: Identify Problem

    Example:

    Users take too long to complete case reviews.

    Step 2: Create Hypothesis

    A hypothesis is a testable prediction.

    Formula:

    If we change X, then Y will improve because Z.

    Example:

    If we provide AI-generated case summaries, then medical reviewers will complete reviews faster because they spend less time reading documents.

    Step 3: Define Success Metrics

    Examples:

    Primary metric:

    Average review time.

    Secondary metrics:

    • Accuracy

    • User satisfaction

    • AI acceptance rate

    Step 4: Split Users

    Example:

    1000 users:

    500 → Version A

    500 → Version B

    Step 5: Analyze Results

    Compare:

    Did B perform better than A?

    Step 6: Decide

    Possible outcomes:

    1. Launch B

    2. Improve and test again

    3. Keep A
